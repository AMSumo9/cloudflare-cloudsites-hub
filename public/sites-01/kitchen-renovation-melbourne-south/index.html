Kitchen Renovation Melbourne South Explained

The Melbourne South Kitchen Renovation guide begins with practical failures such as doors that will not stay shut, insufficient bench space or a layout that no longer suits the household. Those observations are more useful than choosing finishes first, because they identify whether the project needs better storage, a different work pattern or a broader rebuild.

In Melbourne's south east and outer east, homes and block sizes vary, so the right arrangement needs to be worked out room by room. A cramped kitchen may benefit from more usable bench space and drawers, while a larger room may allow tall pantry storage or a butler's pantry. The brief should explain how the household cooks, where appliances sit and which parts of the current room are failing.

  • Record doors, drawers, bench areas and storage that no longer work.
  • Note whether plumbing, gas or electrical positions may need to move.
  • Decide whether a laundry or vanity should be considered in the same project.

Choose the scope before choosing finishes

A full renovation can remove older cabinets, revise the layout, install new cabinetry and benchtops, and coordinate the plumbing, gas, electrical, tiling and painting work behind and around them. The scope can also be narrower, such as fitting flat pack cabinets properly, or more tailored, using custom joinery and natural stone.

Flat pack and custom cabinetry are not simply good and bad versions of the same choice. Flat pack may suit a straightforward room and a tightly controlled budget. Custom joinery can be shaped to the room, including tall pantry walls, a butler's pantry, or doors and drawers made for the available space. Natural stone adds another material decision, with the sink, cooktop and splashback planned into the layout from the start.

Ask for cabinets, benchtops, appliances, plumbing, electrical work, tiling and painting to be identified separately. A written scope makes it easier to compare quotes and see whether an allowance has been made for hidden conditions in an older home.

Understand the order of work

Trade sequencing affects both the programme and the period without a working kitchen. Plumbing and electrical rough-in happen before walls close up, cabinets are installed after the room is ready, and tiling is finished later in the sequence. Moving services or adding new walls can extend the work compared with keeping the existing arrangement.

Once old cabinets are removed, the walls and pipework are easier to inspect. A written scope cannot remove every unknown in an older house, but it can state what is included and how unexpected work will be handled. Custom cabinets also add lead time before installation, so the schedule should distinguish design, manufacture, preparation and fitting.

  1. Confirm the layout and service positions.
  2. Approve the written scope and material allowances.
  3. Coordinate rough-in work before cabinets and walls are closed.
  4. Check the installation sequence through cabinetry, benchtops, tiling and painting.

Compare the two main cabinet approaches

The best choice depends on the room, the budget and how precisely the storage needs to fit. A compact kitchen with a usable existing layout may need efficient cabinets and drawers rather than a complete structural change. A room with awkward dimensions or a strong storage requirement may justify joinery designed around the walls.

Do not compare only the cabinet price. Check whether the quote includes fitting, benchtops, service changes, splashbacks, painting and the trades needed to complete the room. If the project includes natural stone, confirm how the sink and cooktop details are coordinated before fabrication.

Check credentials, certificates and warranty wording

Where cabinetry, plumbing, electrical work and tiling are combined, the quote should name who is responsible for running the job. The published guidance says regulated plumbing and electrical work in Victoria must be completed by licensed trades who issue certificates. It also says a registered building practitioner or building company should run a project that combines these parts.

Warranty questions should separate products from installation. Manufacturer warranties may apply to cabinets, benchtops and appliances, while the contractor's workmanship warranty concerns installation. Australian Consumer Law guarantees also apply to fixed items such as stoves, sinks, tapware and extractor fans once they are permanently part of the building. Ask for the relevant documents and contact details before work begins.

  1. Describe the failures. List the doors, drawers, bench areas, storage problems and layout frustrations that need to change.
  2. Set the project boundary. Decide whether the work includes only the kitchen or also a laundry or vanity.
  3. Review the written scope. Check cabinets, benchtops, services, tiling, painting, allowances and responsibility for each trade.
  4. Confirm the programme. Ask when the kitchen will be unavailable and how the trades move from rough-in to final finishes.

Common questions

What affects the cost of a kitchen renovation in Melbourne's south east? Kitchen size, material choices and whether plumbing or electrical positions move are the main cost factors described in the guide. Flat pack cabinets sit at the lower end, while custom joinery with natural stone sits at the higher end.

How long can the work take? Timing depends on the scope. Keeping the existing layout may finish sooner than moving plumbing or adding walls, and custom cabinets add lead time before installation. A written schedule should state how long the household may be without a working kitchen.

What should a renovation quote include? The quote should identify the fittings and trades, including cabinets, benchtops, plumbing, electrical work, tiling and painting where applicable. It should also state allowances for hidden issues that may appear in an older home.

Who should run a combined kitchen project in Victoria? The published guidance says a registered building practitioner or building company should run work combining cabinetry, plumbing, electrical and tiling. The quote should name the responsible person or company.

What warranty documents should be checked? Ask separately about manufacturer warranties for products, the contractor's workmanship warranty for installation, and Australian Consumer Law guarantees applying to fixed items such as stoves, sinks, tapware and extractor fans.
