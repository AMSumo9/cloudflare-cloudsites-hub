Who this guide applies to

This guide is for owners of older Adelaide homes with ceramic fuses, an outdated or crowded board, repeated circuit faults, or planned electrical additions. It is also relevant when a home is being prepared for solar, a battery, an electric vehicle charger, ducted heating or induction cooking.

The suitable outcome may be a repair, a safety switch installation, a ceramic fuse box upgrade or a full replacement. Repeated tripping does not prove that the board must be replaced. The electrician needs to determine whether the source is the board, wiring or an appliance, then explain the appropriate scope.

Use the first enquiry to define the job

A useful first enquiry gives the electrician enough information to distinguish a fault investigation from a planned upgrade. The following template can be copied and completed without guessing what the board needs:

Property: [suburb or postcode]. Board: [ceramic fuses, breakers, crowded board or unsure]. Symptom: [which circuit trips or what fuse blows]. Timing: [when it happens and what was operating]. Planned additions: [solar, battery, EV charger, ducted heating, induction cooking or none]. Visible concerns: [buzzing, burning smell, hot power point, asbestos backing panel or none known].

Ask for the assessment to state whether the recommendation is repair, safety switch installation, partial work or full replacement. Also ask whether the existing wiring, supply and available space were inspected. These answers create a usable record of why the work was recommended.

What the replacement should address

A full replacement removes an old fuse or breaker panel and installs a modern board with individual circuit protection and safety switches. Ceramic fuse wire must be replaced after it blows, while a breaker can be reset after the underlying fault has been dealt with. A safety switch cuts power quickly when current leaks, adding protection that may be missing from an older arrangement.

The quote should identify whether circuits will be tested and labelled, whether safety switches are included and whether old wiring behind the board needs attention. Older boards can have asbestos backing panels. If that is possible, removal and handling should be planned within the work rather than discovered after the job has started.

Possible supply changes also belong in the discussion. The electrician may need to consider three phase power or a mains upgrade, depending on the proposed work and the existing supply.

Future loads and the quote

A board that works for present demand may need a different layout or supply assessment when the home adds solar, a battery, an EV charger, ducted heating or induction cooking. These additions can require extra circuits or capacity. Spare positions provide room for future circuits, but an empty position alone does not confirm that the supply can support another large appliance.

A clear quote should state the planned work and price before the supply is disconnected. It should explain whether the job is a complete replacement, a safety switch installation, fault repair or a combination. Ask how possible asbestos, old wiring and supply changes could affect the scope, and how concealed problems or variations will be explained.

The power goes off for a period while the old board is removed and the new one installed. The duration depends on the board and wiring behind it. Electrical installation work is performed by a licensed electrician in South Australia, who provides a Certificate of Compliance for the completed work.

When to stop and seek urgent help

A burning smell, buzzing or hot power points near the board should be reported to an electrician. If smoke or sparks are visible, call 000. Repeated tripping should be investigated rather than repeatedly resetting the breaker without finding the cause.

For a normal appointment, prepare the circuit name if it is labelled, describe what was operating when the fault occurred and list planned additions. The electrician can then assess the immediate issue alongside the work the home may need next.

  1. Describe the symptom. Record which circuit trips or whether a ceramic fuse blows, and note what was operating at the time.
  2. List planned loads. Mention solar, a battery, an EV charger, ducted heating, induction cooking or other additions being considered.
  3. Request a site assessment. Ask the electrician to inspect the board, wiring, supply and available space before recommending repair or replacement.
  4. Check the quote. Confirm the planned components, testing, labelling, possible asbestos handling and treatment of concealed problems.
  5. Collect compliance paperwork. Ask for the Certificate of Compliance once the completed upgrade has been tested.

Common questions

Will the power go off during the work? Yes. The supply is isolated while the old board is changed, so lights and appliances stop for a period. The duration depends on the board and wiring behind it.

Does repeated tripping always mean the switchboard needs replacement? No. The cause may be the board, old wiring or an appliance. An on site investigation helps determine whether repair or replacement is appropriate.

Can a new board support a future EV charger or induction cooktop? It may provide room for additional circuits, but spare space alone does not confirm that the supply can support a large appliance. The electrician needs to assess the supply and proposed load.

What happens if the board has an asbestos backing panel? Possible asbestos should be identified during planning so removal and handling can be included in the work scope.

What paperwork should follow the upgrade? The electrician provides a Certificate of Compliance for the completed electrical work.
