Private Lending Australia Explained

Bottom Line Finance presents private lending as commercial, business-purpose capital from non-bank and private lenders, arranged for developers and asset-backed borrowers who do not fit standard bank settings. The borrower is usually solving a deal constraint, settlement timing, trapped equity, construction funding, residual stock, or a capital stack gap.

The published guide draws a clear line between price and value. A facility can be poor fit even when the rate looks attractive if it delays the project, leaves equity locked up, or weakens the next transaction. The sharper comparison is cost of capital beside return on equity, timing, risk and the future funding pathway.

It also states that the provider arranges finance through third-party non-bank and private lenders, and does not arrange consumer credit or owner-occupied home loans.

Who This Applies To

This guide is relevant to borrowers with a property-backed deal, a commercial or business-purpose use of funds, and a structure that may not suit a mainstream lender. The named facility types include development funding, construction funding, bridging finance, mezzanine capital, second mortgages, preferred equity, caveat loans and residual stock loans.

  • Developers testing land, build costs, draw timing and cost to completion.
  • Asset-backed borrowers using residential, commercial, industrial, specialised, childcare, rural or agribusiness security.
  • Completed-stock borrowers considering refinance after practical completion.
  • Borrowers with a timing gap between settlement, sale, equity release or project completion.

How To Frame The Request

A useful enquiry is built around the project, the numbers, the asset and the timeline. The published contact prompt asks borrowers to send the project, numbers and timeline because assessment depends on the security stack and the commercial reason for the request.

For construction and development borrowers, prepare more than a funding amount. The relevant questions are how the land, build, cost to completion and draw program fit together. Construction funding is described as using draws released as work is completed. Development funding is described as funding the land, build and cost to completion.

For bridging, name the timing gap and the event expected to repay the facility. For residual stock, the trigger is completed unsold dwellings after practical completion, where refinancing may release equity tied up in finished stock.

Capital Stack Questions

Private capital decisions can be confused when every offer is treated as if it sits in the same risk position. Senior debt, mezzanine debt, preferred equity and second mortgage positions can solve different problems inside the same project.

Mezzanine finance is described as a subordinated layer of debt behind the senior lender and ahead of equity. That affects priority, repayment, control and exit planning. A borrower should ask how the proposed layer works with any senior facility, what information the lender needs, and what would trigger repayment.

Preferred equity and second mortgages need the same discipline. Ask where the money sits, which asset secures it, what event repays it, and whether the structure preserves enough equity for the current project and the next deal.

Asset Fit And Evidence

The published page names residential, commercial, industrial, specialised assets, childcare, and farm and agribusiness as areas where funding may be arranged. Those labels are starting points, not approval tests. The deal and the security still need to stack up.

Different assets call for different evidence. Specialised assets and social infrastructure may require attention to income, operator and long-term value. Rural and agribusiness deals are described as rarely fitting a standard bank box. Industrial property is described as a sought-after asset class for structured funding, supported by tenant demand in many markets.

Before speaking with a finance broker, prepare asset type, ownership structure, existing debt, valuation position if available, intended use of funds, exit strategy and timing pressure. That gives a lender enough context to decide whether private capital belongs in the conversation.

Questions Before Proceeding

A good funding conversation should produce clear next steps, not just a possible approval. Ask what type of capital is being proposed, which lender type is likely to assess it, what security is required, how funds are advanced, what could delay release, and what event repays the facility.

  • Is the borrowing clearly commercial or business-purpose?
  • Which asset or assets secure the facility?
  • Where does the money sit in the capital stack?
  • Does the structure protect equity or only fill a short-term gap?
  • What information is missing before a lender can assess the request?

ASIC Moneysmart is useful background for general home loan and property investment concepts. The published page also says its information is general only and is not financial, credit or investment advice.

  1. Define the purpose. State the commercial or business-purpose use of funds before discussing a facility.
  2. Map the security. List the asset backing the loan, existing debt and intended exit.
  3. Compare value. Review rate, timing, equity impact and future deal capacity together.
  4. Clarify priority. Ask where the funds sit relative to senior debt and equity.
  5. Check the boundary. Confirm the request is not consumer credit or an owner-occupied home loan.

Common questions

Is this the same as an ordinary home loan? No. The published page frames the service as commercial and business-purpose funding arranged through third-party non-bank and private lenders, not consumer credit or owner-occupied home loans.

What should a borrower prepare first? Prepare the project, numbers, asset type and timeline. For construction or development, include the build position, cost to completion and intended exit where available.

Does the lowest rate mean the best option? Not always. The published page says the cheapest rate is rarely the best outcome and weighs cost of capital against return on equity, time, risk and future relationships.

Can private capital apply outside residential development? Yes. The page names residential, commercial, industrial, specialised assets, childcare, farm and agribusiness. Fit still depends on the deal and security.
