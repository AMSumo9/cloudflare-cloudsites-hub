Questions to ask before relying on a recommendation

Australian mortgage brokers are required to act in the borrower's best interests when providing credit assistance. The source page also says broker credentials should be disclosed in writing before a customer commits. A borrower can use that disclosure stage to slow the process down and check whether the recommendation is understandable.

Useful questions are direct. How is the broker paid, and does any fee apply to this scenario? Which lenders were considered, and why were others ruled out? If the recommendation is for fixed, variable, interest-only, principal and interest, bridging or construction finance, what trade-offs were considered? If debt consolidation is involved, what happens to the total debt position after refinancing?

ASIC Moneysmart explains home loans in terms of affordability, costs and comparing options, which is a helpful frame for reviewing any broker recommendation. The borrower still needs to read the credit proposal, understand repayments and check whether the loan purpose matches the product features.

How to compare broker help with going direct

Going straight to a bank can suit borrowers who already know the lender they want and have a simple application. The limitation is that a bank offers its own loans. The broker route is designed for comparison across multiple lenders, with the reason for the recommendation explained in writing.

The source page says the right loan rarely comes down to the headline rate. That is a useful discipline for borrowers. A low rate can still be the wrong fit if the lender's servicing treatment, document requirements, construction process or investment policy does not suit the file. The decision should be based on suitability, total costs, timing and confidence that the loan can reach approval.

Before proceeding, gather payslips or income records, current loan statements, savings evidence, contract details if buying, building information if constructing, and a clear explanation of the goal. Better inputs make the comparison more useful.

  1. Define the goal. State whether the loan is for a first purchase, refinance, investment, construction, bridging or debt consolidation before comparing products.
  2. Prepare evidence. Collect income records, existing loan details, savings evidence and property information so serviceability can be assessed realistically.
  3. Compare options. Ask for the reasoning behind lender selection, including why unsuitable options were excluded.
  4. Review disclosure. Read how the broker is paid, whether any fee applies and which credit licence or accreditation details are disclosed.
  5. Proceed only when clear. Move to application when the repayment, structure, timing and product trade-offs are understood.

Common questions

Does using a broker mean the loan is automatically cheaper? No. The source page frames broker value as comparison, serviceability fit and guidance, not a guarantee of the cheapest loan. Borrowers should compare rate, fees, structure and suitability.

Can first home buyers use this type of service? Yes. The source page specifically lists first home buyer loans and says deposit options, the First Home Guarantee and NSW concessions are explained for the Illawarra market.

What should a refinancer prepare before asking for help? Prepare current loan statements, repayment details, the reason for refinancing and whether the goal includes debt consolidation or equity release. That gives the broker enough context to compare options.

Are self-employed borrowers included? Yes. The source page refers to alt-doc and full-doc pathways for ABN holders, contractors and trades workers, so income evidence should be discussed early.
