Kitchen Renovation Melbourne Explained

Melbourne Kitchen Reno describes a free in-home design consult followed by a fixed-price written quote. That order suits kitchen work because measurements, appliance positions, cabinet runs, benchtop choice, plumbing changes and electrical updates all affect the final price.

Use the first conversation to separate wants from assumptions. A full rebuild with new cabinetry, benchtops, plumbing, electrical updates, splashback and soft-close hardware is not the same brief as a refresh that keeps the layout. Custom joinery for a period terrace, Edwardian or bungalow is also a different quoting exercise from a simpler replacement.

  • Ask what is included in demolition, manufacture, installation and handover.
  • Confirm whether soft-close runners and hinges are included in the base price.
  • Ask how variations are approved and priced after signing.
  • Check that the quote reflects the actual home, not only a showroom layout.

Budget signals that change the job

The published cost guide gives three useful bands: a budget refresh at about $12,000 to $30,000, a common fully fitted range of $25,000 to $60,000, and premium projects that can exceed $80,000. Treat those numbers as a sorting tool, then ask why your brief belongs in one band.

Layout change is often the dividing line. Keeping services in similar positions can keep the brief simpler. Moving plumbing, changing appliance locations or adding a butler's pantry adds design and trade coordination. Natural stone, custom joinery, premium hardware and larger storage zones also push a project upward.

For owners still shaping the brief, a related kitchen design Melbourne guide can help organise layout questions before the renovation quote is tested against site conditions.

Who this applies to

This guide is for Melbourne homeowners replacing more than a few surfaces and comparing renovators on scope, timing and contract clarity. It also suits owners of small and apartment kitchens, where storage, bench space and owners corporation documentation can affect the brief.

The published guide names compact galley, single-wall and apartment kitchens as common requests across inner Melbourne, with Richmond, Brunswick, Footscray and the inner city given as examples. The useful question is whether the renovator understands compact layouts, access constraints, acoustic underlay where relevant, and any paperwork an owners corporation may request.

Period homes need a different lens. The guide points to period terraces, Edwardians and bungalows as homes where made-to-measure joinery may suit better than a flat-pack run. Ask how the cabinetmaker handles proportion, finish style and storage while keeping the room consistent with the home.

Timing from contract to handover

The published guide gives a mid-range full renovation timeframe of roughly 6 to 12 weeks from contract signing to handover. It breaks that into about 4 to 6 weeks for cabinetry and stone fabrication, 2 to 4 weeks of site work once cabinetry is available, and 1 to 2 weeks for finishing.

Those stages explain why a kitchen can feel slow before demolition starts. Cabinets, benchtops and appliances need to be specified early enough for ordering and manufacture. A useful timeline should state when final measurements happen, when selections are locked, when the old kitchen comes out, and when the room is usable again.

  • Before signing, ask which items have the longest lead time.
  • Before demolition, confirm appliance delivery dates and access arrangements.
  • During install, keep variation approvals in writing.
  • At handover, check hardware, splashback, benchtops, cabinetry alignment and finishing items.

Registration, contracts and consult questions

The published offer refers to VBA-registered renovators, HIA or Master Builders accreditation, fixed-price written contracts and domestic building insurance where the contract value requires it. Ask who is responsible for design, manufacture, demolition and installation, and who approves changes after work starts.

For a kitchen renovation melbourne brief, the written scope should name the cabinet finish, benchtop material, splashback, hardware, plumbing and electrical allowances, plus exclusions. Small design changes can affect multiple trades and the project calendar, so the variation process should be clear before signing.

A free in-home consult is more productive when you bring photos, a rough budget, must-keep appliances, storage problems and finish preferences. For apartment kitchens, ask early about owners corporation documentation. For larger homes, ask whether a butler's pantry is realistic for the floor plan and budget.

  1. Define the brief. Write down the current kitchen problems, preferred layout changes, must-keep appliances and a rough budget before requesting a consult.
  2. Check project fit. Ask whether the renovator regularly handles your property type, such as a period home, apartment kitchen or compact layout.
  3. Test the quote. Compare inclusions, exclusions, material allowances, hardware, trade work and variation rules before treating any price as comparable.
  4. Lock the sequence. Confirm fabrication lead times, demolition timing, site work dates and finishing checks before signing the contract.
