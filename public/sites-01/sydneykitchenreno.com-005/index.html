Use scope to control price drift

The published inclusions for a full renovation are specific, new cabinetry, stone benchtops, plumbing and electrical updates, splashback work and soft-close hardware. The same page says the builder runs design, manufacture, demolition and installation on a fixed-price contract, so the first quote should show whether those items are actually inside the scope you are approving.

The cost range is broad because the source page spans budget refreshes, standard full renovations and premium custom rebuilds with layout changes. It also names the main price drivers, layout changes, custom joinery, premium stone, appliance selection, strata or access constraints, and heritage or structural work. Use the calculator as an indicative starting point, then test the fixed-price quote against those drivers.

  • Check whether demolition is included.
  • Check whether plumbing and electrical updates are included.
  • Ask which finish choices move the price fastest.

When the property type changes the questions

The source page separates heritage and Federation kitchen rebuilds from strata-approved apartment kitchens for a reason. Character homes are described as needing careful joinery, service upgrades and finishes that respect the age of the house. Apartment projects are framed around body-corp documentation, plumbing through party walls, acoustic underlay and after-hours work windows.

Those are not styling details, they change what you need clarified before the quote is accepted. In an older house, the question is how service upgrades and joinery decisions will be handled before manufacture starts. In an apartment, the question is what approvals, bookings and documents must be in place before the builder can begin.

  • Older homes, ask about joinery and service-upgrade planning.
  • Apartments, ask what paperwork and access approvals are needed.
  • Tight sites, ask what could delay delivery or installation.

Read the timeline in stages

The source page gives timing in layers, and that is more useful than one headline date. A typical project is described as taking about 6 to 12 weeks from sign-off to handover, including design, quoting, cabinetry fabrication, benchtop templating and on-site work. Separately, site work is said to take about 2 to 4 weeks once cabinetry is on site.

That means the better planning question is not only when trades arrive. It is when the brief is approved, when the fixed-price quote is signed, when fabrication starts and whether site access is ready. For apartment work, the FAQ says approvals, lift bookings, waste rules, working-hour limits, and acoustic or waterproofing documentation can all slow the start.

If timing matters, ask the builder to walk through those stages in order rather than treating the job as one block of time.

What the contract should settle

The published build step is clear about the paperwork, fixed-price HIA or Master Builders contract, transparent variations process, and NSW home building compensation cover included. That gives owners a straightforward checklist for the approval stage, because the contract should settle scope, price basis and how changes are recorded before demolition starts.

This matters most when layout changes, access limits or older-home upgrades are still in play. If the quote is fixed price, ask which items are fixed, which assumptions sit behind the price, and how a variation is documented if site conditions or selections change after sign-off.

  • Confirm the contract form being used.
  • Confirm how variations are priced and approved.
  • Confirm what is included before materials are ordered.
