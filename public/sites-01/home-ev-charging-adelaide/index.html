Home Ev Charging Adelaide Explained

Adelaide EV Charger Installation identifies the switchboard as the first practical check. A wall charger draws power for hours, so the board must carry it alongside existing loads such as an oven, air conditioner or pool pump. The electrician can then determine whether the charger needs its own protected circuit.

Parking position also shapes the work. A garage wall, side wall or carport can require a different cable route, especially when the board is on the far side of the house or garage. Before requesting a price, provide the parking location, switchboard position, vehicle details and charger model if one has already been purchased.

  • Ask whether the board has room for a protected circuit.
  • Identify any older ceramic fuses or other board limitations.
  • Confirm how the cable will reach the charging position.

Match charging speed to the car and supply

A dedicated Level 2 wall charger is designed to run for hours and is quicker than a standard powerpoint. Its useful speed still depends on the home's supply and the vehicle's charging capability. Selecting a larger unit without checking the car can leave capacity unused.

Three phase power can support a wall charger of up to 22kW where the vehicle can accept it. That choice should be considered with the switchboard, supply and cable route. A dedicated 15A powerpoint in a garage or carport provides a slower, budget oriented way to charge overnight, which may suit a vehicle parked for long periods.

Ask for the expected charging arrangement in writing, including the proposed unit, circuit and any assumption about the vehicle's onboard charging capacity.

Plan shared sites and existing energy systems

Apartment charging introduces a shared supply question. In a strata car park, the body corporate is usually part of the discussion. Load control can allow several residents to charge without overloading the building's main switch. Owners should establish who approves the work, where the cable will run and how charging demand will be managed.

Workplaces need a plan based on parking duration and vehicle turnover. All day staff parking can suit slower AC chargers, while a fleet that turns around quickly may need faster DC units. Staggered charging and load management should be considered before equipment is selected.

Solar panels or a home battery can also be included in the assessment. Ask whether the charger can work alongside those systems and whether that changes the proposed circuit or control arrangement.

Use the quote to define the actual job

A useful written quote separates the charger installation from work that depends on the property. Cable length, board position, spare capacity and switchboard condition can all alter the scope. If an older board has ceramic fuses or no room for the charger, an upgrade may be needed and should appear as its own line before work begins.

  • Confirm the charger model and proposed circuit.
  • Check whether protection and switchboard work are included.
  • Ask how the cable route will be installed.
  • Request likely timing after the site has been assessed.

If the charger has already been bought, ask the electrician to confirm that it suits both the car and the home's supply. Comparing complete scopes is more useful than comparing a bare installation label.

Confirm completion and South Australian requirements

The published guidance says the completed job should include an electronic Certificate of Compliance. It describes that certificate as a legal document under the Electricity Act 1996 and says the work must be completed by appropriately licensed and registered people.

It also states that, from 1 July 2024, in scope AC wall chargers in South Australia must meet the Technical Regulator's EVSE standard, including demand response settings. Connecting a Remote Agent, described as the grid's remote control, requires agreement at installation.

Before approving the work, ask how those settings apply to the selected charger. Keep the certificate, final scope and charger information with the home's electrical records, particularly where the property has solar, a battery, shared parking or three phase power.

  1. Describe the site. Give the electrician the parking position, switchboard location, vehicle details and nearby equipment that may affect the work.
  2. Check the supply. Have the switchboard, existing loads and available space assessed before choosing the charger or accepting a quote.
  3. Compare the scope. Review the cable route, charger circuit, protection and any switchboard upgrade as separate parts of the written price.
  4. Confirm compliance. Ask how the charger will meet current South Australian settings and ensure an electronic Certificate of Compliance is issued when the job is finished.

Common questions

Can a regular electrician install a home charger? A licensed electrician can carry out electrical work on a home's wiring. The published guidance recommends choosing someone who regularly fits chargers, assesses the switchboard, follows the current South Australian guideline and issues the Certificate of Compliance.

How long does a home charger installation take? The duration depends on the cable run and switchboard. A short run to a board with spare room differs from a long run or board upgrade, so likely timing is confirmed after the site has been seen.

Is a 22kW charger suitable for every home or car? No. The guidance links a charger of up to 22kW with three phase power and says it is useful only where the vehicle can accept the extra charging power. The supply and vehicle should be checked together.

What happens if the switchboard is old? Older boards with ceramic fuses may have no spare room for a charger. Their condition and capacity should be assessed before the price is set, with any required upgrade shown separately.

What should apartment owners ask first? Ask how the body corporate will be involved, where the cable will run and how load control will prevent several residents from overloading the shared main supply.

Can a purchased charger be installed? It may be possible, but the electrician should confirm that the unit suits the vehicle and the home's electrical supply before installation.
