The published approach from Melbourne South Kitchen Renovation begins with what is failing now: drawers that no longer close, doors that will not stay shut, limited bench space or a layout that does not suit the household. That gives the first conversation a useful starting point instead of treating every kitchen as the same job.

Choose the scope before choosing finishes

A full renewal can involve removing old cabinets, planning the layout, installing new benchtops, and coordinating plumbing, gas, electrical work, tiling and painting. Smaller work may focus on compact storage, additional bench space or replacement cabinetry. The choice between flat pack and custom joinery should follow the room, the required fit and the available budget.

Custom cabinetry can be built around the room, including tall pantry storage or a butler's pantry. Flat pack cabinets may suit a simpler configuration when they are fitted correctly. Natural stone brings another design decision because the sink, cooktop and splashback need to be planned with the benchtop layout from the beginning.

Read a quote as a sequence of work

The order of trades affects both the schedule and the risk of omissions. Plumbing and electrical rough-in happen before walls close up, cabinets are installed before the final benchtop work, and tiling is completed later in the sequence. A quote should make clear which person or company is responsible for each stage.

  1. Confirm the layout, storage requirements and any service positions that will move.
  2. List cabinets, benchtops, appliances, splashbacks, painting and tiling as separate scope items.
  3. Identify who handles plumbing, gas and electrical work, and ask for the relevant certificates.
  4. Set out allowances for hidden conditions that may only appear after old cabinets are removed.
  5. Request a written schedule showing how long the kitchen may be unavailable.

Older homes can reveal wall or pipework conditions only after demolition. A written scope cannot remove every unknown, but it gives the homeowner a clear basis for comparing inclusions and allowances.

Compare the two main cabinet paths

There is no single best cabinet choice. The useful comparison is how closely the system fits the room, how much adjustment the design needs, and whether the benchtop and storage decisions depend on custom dimensions.

Check approvals, certificates and warranties

In Victoria, the published guidance says regulated plumbing and electrical work must be completed by licensed trades who issue certificates. A quote should identify who is doing each regulated part. For a project combining cabinetry, plumbing, electrical work and tiling, the provider advises checking that a registered building practitioner or building company is responsible for running the job.

Warranty questions should separate products from installation. Manufacturer warranties may apply to cabinets, benchtops and appliances, while a contractor's workmanship warranty relates to installation. Australian Consumer Law guarantees also apply to fixed items such as stoves, sinks, tapware and extractor fans once they form part of the building. Ask where each warranty sits and who handles a problem.

The practical finish is a written record of the agreed scope, price, schedule, certificates and warranty contacts. That record is more useful than relying on a verbal description of what the finished kitchen will include.

  1. Describe the current problem. Note failing drawers, poor storage, limited bench space and any access issue near the work area.
  2. Set the functional brief. Explain how the household cooks, what must be stored and whether a laundry or vanity should be considered.
  3. Confirm the layout. Ask whether plumbing, gas or electrical positions move and what that change does to the sequence.
  4. Compare written scopes. Check cabinets, benchtops, trades, allowances, certificates, warranties and the proposed time without a working kitchen.
  5. Approve the documented plan. Proceed only when the agreed work, price and responsibilities are clear in writing.
