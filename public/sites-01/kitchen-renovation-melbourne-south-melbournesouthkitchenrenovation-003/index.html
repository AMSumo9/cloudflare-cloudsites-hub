Choose cabinetry and surfaces by need

Flat pack cabinets may suit a simpler room and controlled budget. Custom cabinetry is built around the room's dimensions and can accommodate tall pantry walls, a butler's pantry, drawers or awkward spaces. Ask what must be decided before ordering, including measurements, hardware and storage priorities.

Natural stone benchtops require the sink, cooktop and splashback to be planned into the layout from the beginning. That choice belongs beside appliance and service decisions, rather than being left until installation. A smaller kitchen may prioritise usable bench length and drawer access, while a larger room may benefit from more tailored joinery.

Check handover documents and warranties

For work combining cabinetry, plumbing, electrical work and tiling, identify the registered building practitioner or building company responsible for running the project. Ask who coordinates the trades and which certificates, product documents and approvals will be handed over.

Manufacturer warranties may cover cabinets, benchtops and appliances. A contractor's workmanship warranty relates to installation, while Australian Consumer Law guarantees apply to fixed items such as stoves, sinks, tapware and extractor fans once permanently part of the building.

Keep the written scope, selections, variation approvals, certificates and warranty documents together. Before sign-off, check that doors and drawers operate, agreed fittings are present and visible finishes match the selections. Record unfinished items in writing and ask when each will be completed.

  3. Check the sequence. Ask for the order of removal, rough-in, cabinetry, benchtop installation, tiling and painting.
  4. Verify responsibility. Have the quote name the responsible practitioner or company, licensed plumbing and electrical trades, certificates and warranties.
  5. Review handover. Compare the finished room with the written scope and record outstanding work before closing the project.
