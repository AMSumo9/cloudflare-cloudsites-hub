Check the work behind the cabinets

A full renovation may involve cabinetry, benchtops, plumbing, gas, electrical work, tiling and painting. Moving a sink, cooktop or appliance can change the work behind the walls and affect both the quote and the order of trades.

Removing old cabinets makes walls and pipework easier to inspect. Older homes can reveal conditions that were not visible at the quoting stage, so the written scope should explain how hidden issues will be handled and whether an allowance applies. This gives the homeowner a defined process for discussing variations.

Rough-in work for pipes and cables comes before cabinet installation, with finishing work later. The quote should identify the licensed trades responsible for regulated plumbing and electrical work and state which certificates will be supplied. Where cabinetry, plumbing, electrical work and tiling form one project, check which registered building practitioner or building company coordinates the job.

  • Confirm whether plumbing or electrical positions are moving.
  • Ask which items are supplied, installed or excluded.
  • Keep approvals for changes with the original scope.

Choose cabinetry and surfaces by use

Flat pack and custom cabinetry suit different rooms. Flat pack cabinets may suit a controlled budget and straightforward layout. Custom joinery can respond more closely to awkward walls, tall pantry storage or a butler's pantry. Compare the storage result, not just the cabinet material.

Natural stone adds a separate coordination question. The sink, cooktop and splashback need to be planned with the benchtop because the surface is cut to the layout. Confirm these positions before approving fabrication.

In a small kitchen, usable bench space, drawers and storage may matter more than additional features that reduce circulation. A larger room can accommodate more joinery, but preparation, cooking and cleaning areas still need to relate sensibly. If a laundry or vanity is included, ask for its work to be shown separately so the kitchen allowance remains clear.

Compare the quote with the schedule

Quotes can differ because their scopes differ. Compare cabinet numbers, benchtop material, handles, appliances, demolition, waste removal, plumbing, electrical work, tiling, painting and installation. A lower figure may exclude a trade or leave an allowance unclear.

The schedule should show removal of the existing kitchen, rough-in work, cabinet arrival and the expected period without a working kitchen. Keeping the layout generally finishes sooner than moving plumbing or adding walls. Custom cabinets can add lead time before installation, so those dates should appear in the programme.

Before approving the work, ask:

  • What is the agreed price for each fitting and trade?
  • What happens if hidden wall or pipework conditions are found?
  • Which dates depend on cabinet manufacture, stone templating or appliance delivery?
  • Who records changes and confirms the revised price?

Who this guide applies to

This guide suits homeowners planning a full replacement, a small update, a layout change or a project that also includes a laundry or vanity. It is also relevant when deciding between flat pack cabinets and custom joinery, or considering natural stone.

It is most useful where the existing room has failing doors, limited bench space, poor storage or a layout designed for a different household. It should not be treated as a fixed price promise, because the final cost depends on size, materials and service positions.

Before work begins, check the named practitioner or company, written scope, trade responsibilities, certificates for regulated work, product warranties and workmanship warranty. Australian Consumer Law guarantees apply to fixed items such as stoves, sinks, tapware and extractor fans. Keep the design, quote, schedule and approved variations together.

  1. Record the problem. List the doors, storage, bench space and layout issues that need to change.
  2. Map the room. Confirm walls, access, appliances, plumbing, gas and electrical positions before choosing finishes.
  3. Choose the build level. Compare flat pack cabinets, custom joinery and benchtop materials against the room and budget.
  4. Compare written scopes. Check fittings, trades, allowances, exclusions, certificates and variation procedures.
  5. Approve the sequence. Make sure rough-in work, cabinets, benchtops, tiling and painting appear in the schedule.

Common questions

What affects renovation cost? The main factors are kitchen size, materials, and whether plumbing or electrical positions move. Flat pack cabinetry is described at the lower end, while custom joinery with natural stone is at the higher end.

How does the scope affect timing? Keeping the existing layout generally finishes sooner than moving plumbing or adding walls. Custom cabinets can add lead time before installation, so request a schedule showing when the kitchen will be unavailable.

Should rough-in work happen before cabinets? Yes. Plumbing and electrical rough-in comes before cabinet installation, with tiling later in the programme. The quote should identify the licensed trades and certificates involved.

What should an older home allowance address? Removing old cabinets can reveal wall and pipework conditions that were not visible earlier. The written scope should explain how those issues will be assessed and priced.

What warranty details should be checked? Manufacturer warranties may cover cabinets, benchtops and appliances, while a workmanship warranty covers installation. Australian Consumer Law guarantees apply to fixed items including stoves, sinks, tapware and extractor fans.
