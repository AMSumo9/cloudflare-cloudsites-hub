Repair, adjustment or replacement?

A repair first assessment helps separate a localised fault from a component or door that needs replacing. A roller or track adjustment may be a narrower job than replacing a spring or motor, while full replacement is a separate decision involving door type, opener compatibility and installation. The appropriate comparison depends on the inspection and written scope.

Ask which component failed, what was tested, and whether other relevant parts were checked. If replacement is suggested, ask why repair is not the sensible option and whether the quote includes removal, installation, setup and testing. These questions help compare the work without assuming that every noisy or slow door needs a new system.

For a door that still operates, describe the noise or movement clearly. For a door that will not open, reverses, or has left its guides, describe exactly what happens when the control is used. A precise symptom gives the technician a better starting point for checking springs, openers, rollers, panels and tracks.

What to include in a quote request

A concise enquiry should contain four practical details: the suburb, the door style, the visible or audible symptom, and the preferred contact timing. The published form asks for a name, email, suburb or postcode, a description of what is happening and a best time to call, with options covering business hours and Saturday morning.

  • Identify whether the door is roller or sectional, if known.
  • Describe whether it reverses, sticks, rattles, will not open, or has left its guides.
  • Mention whether the issue concerns a spring, opener, motor, remote, sensor, curtain, drum, guide, panel or track.
  • Ask for the repair scope and price in writing before parts or replacement work begins.

The website states that the door type and fault are confirmed on site before a written, fixed price quote is provided. Compare that written scope with the problem reported, and ask about anything absent or unclear before approving the work.
