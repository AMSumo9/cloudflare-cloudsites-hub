Ev Charger Installation Melbourne Explained

The guide published by Ev Charger Installation Melbourne begins with the place where the car will actually sit. A garage, carport and open driveway can each change the mounting method, cable route and amount of work required. Measure the approximate distance to the switchboard and note the wall type before requesting a quote.

Older Melbourne weatherboard and brick homes may have switchboards that were not designed around overnight vehicle charging. Newer estate homes may already have a more suitable arrangement, but that still needs to be checked rather than assumed. A useful enquiry includes the suburb, property type, parking location, charger model if known, and whether solar or a battery is connected.

  • Photograph the parking area and the switchboard.
  • Record whether the supply appears single-phase or three-phase.
  • Identify whether the charger is already purchased or still being selected.

Compare the charger and supply choices

A universal Type 2 charger, a Tesla Wall Connector and a solar-aware unit serve different household situations. The vehicle's onboard charger is an important limit: a three-phase charger can deliver 11 to 22kW, but the car may not use all of that capacity. Paying for a higher output is therefore a decision about the car, the home's electrical supply and the expected charging routine.

Solar changes the timing question. A solar-aware charger can use surplus rooftop generation, while some households also consider cheaper tariff windows. Existing panels, batteries and inverters should be described during the assessment so the proposed installation can be considered alongside the current system.

Where the switchboard cannot simply accept a new dedicated circuit, the discussion may involve an upgrade, load assessment, load management or added protection. Ask the electrician to state which work is included, what remains conditional on inspection, and how the charger will be protected.

Apartments, townhouses and shared parking

Apartment and townhouse installations introduce decisions that do not usually arise at a detached home. A shared car park may require owners corporation approval, separate metering or a load-sharing arrangement. Renters and unit owners should confirm who can approve the cable route, mounting point and connection to common or private electrical infrastructure.

Ask for the proposed meter location, the route from the supply to the parking bay, and the method for recording electricity use. If approval is needed, obtain it before ordering work. A quote that describes only the wall unit may leave out the administrative and electrical steps that determine whether the project can proceed.

What should be in a comparable quote

Two quotes for the same charger can differ because the cable run, mounting spot, switchboard condition and charger type are different. Compare the line items rather than the bottom line alone. The quote should make it possible to see whether the dedicated circuit, cable route, mounting, switchboard work, protection and certification are included.

A practical enquiry sequence

Use the following order to make the first conversation more useful:

  1. Describe the parking bay. State whether the car sits in a garage, carport or open driveway and give the approximate distance to the switchboard.
  2. Describe the electrical supply. Note the apparent switchboard age, main switch, breakers, single-phase or three-phase supply, and any solar or battery equipment.
  3. Name the charger. Provide the model if already purchased, or ask about a universal Type 2 unit, Tesla Wall Connector, three-phase option or solar-aware charger.
  4. Request an itemised quote. Ask what is included for cabling, mounting, switchboard work, protection, approval steps and the Certificate of Electrical Safety.
  5. Check the finished paperwork. Confirm that the required Victorian safety certificate is issued and that any inspection requirement has been addressed.

Common questions

What usually changes the installation price? Cable-run length, mounting location, charger type and switchboard condition are the main variables identified in the guide. Older homes may need additional switchboard work, while some newer homes may only need a dedicated circuit.

Will a three-phase charger always charge the car faster? No. Three-phase home chargers can deliver 11 to 22kW, but the vehicle's onboard charger may not accept the full output. The car's limit should be checked before paying for higher capacity.

What paperwork should a Victorian homeowner receive? The electrician or Registered Electrical Contractor must issue a Certificate of Electrical Safety and provide a copy. Some prescribed work also needs an independent Licensed Electrical Inspector.

Can solar be included in the charging plan? Yes, the installation discussion can consider a solar-aware charger that uses surplus rooftop generation and cheaper tariff windows. Existing panels, batteries and inverters should be disclosed before the design is settled.

What changes for an apartment or townhouse? Shared parking can require owners corporation approval, separate metering and load sharing. Confirm the cable route, parking-bay connection and approval process before ordering the installation.
