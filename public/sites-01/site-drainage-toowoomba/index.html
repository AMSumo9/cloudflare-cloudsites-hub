Separate bulk movement from final preparation

Large sites often need two different earthworks decisions. Bulk work moves soil to reach the design levels. Detailed work then trims and shapes the ground, forms the pad and finishes the fine grades needed by the builder. Treating both as one vague allowance can hide what is included in the price.

Bulk earthworks in Toowoomba are listed at $10,000 to $50,000, while compaction and grading are listed at $2,500 to $10,000 in October 2026. Those figures are guide ranges rather than a project quote. Site size, soil, the distance spoil is carted, imported fill, rock in the cut and testing requirements can change the result.

Plan drainage around the build sequence

The published sequence places bulk cut and fill first, followed by trenches and drainage, then finished grades that the pad and road base depend on. That order matters when a site has a house pad, subdivision road or commercial building area to prepare. The drainage discussion should therefore cover both the installation and the level it must meet when the work is complete.

For a subdivision stage, ask whether roads, drainage, kerb and channel are included or handled separately, and what must be complete before sign off. For a house or commercial pad, clarify how water is directed away from slabs and batters. These questions keep the quote tied to the engineer's drawings rather than to a broad promise to "level the site".

A single scope may include pipes, pits, outfalls and channels alongside excavation, road formation and building-pad preparation. If several trades are involved, write down each handover so the next contractor knows what has been completed and what remains.

What to put in the written quote

A useful quote should let the owner see what happens before, during and after the machine work. Request the scope and price in writing, then check whether it identifies cut and fill, excavation, trenching, drainage, roads, compaction, grading, spoil cartage and any testing or certification. The more clearly the handover is described, the easier it is to identify exclusions.

  1. Provide the engineer's drawings and the site address or suburb.
  2. Describe access, nearby constraints and any known soil or rock information.
  3. Ask which drainage assets are included, including pipes, pits, outfalls and channels.
  4. Confirm who supplies imported fill, removes spoil and arranges testing where conditions require it.
  5. Ask what level, grade and surface condition the next trade receives.

What does site drainage involve on a Toowoomba build? It can include stormwater pipes, pits, outfalls and channels that move water away from slabs and batters. The required arrangement should follow the engineer's drawings and the site's finished grades.
