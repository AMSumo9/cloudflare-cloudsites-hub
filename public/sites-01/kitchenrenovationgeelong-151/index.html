Custom Cabinetry Geelong Explained

The choice of carcass and door material dictates both durability and style in a new kitchen. Moisture-resistant MDF is a staple for budget-conscious renovations, offering a smooth finish that accepts paint well, although it is heavier than plywood and susceptible to swelling if exposed to water. Plywood carcasses provide superior structural strength and lighter weight, making them a preferred choice for premium builds and high-traffic kitchens where longevity is paramount. For the doors, two-pack polyurethane delivers a high-gloss or satin finish that is highly durable and easy to clean, fitting seamlessly into contemporary designs. Alternatively, timber veneer brings the natural warmth of grain patterns suited to Hamptons or coastal styles, but it requires protection from direct water exposure. Solid timber options, while the most expensive, offer unique character and the ability to be refinished, making them a prestige choice for heritage homes. When evaluating these custom cabinetry options, consider the long-term maintenance alongside the initial investment.

Understanding Cost Structures

Pricing is determined by linear metres and the complexity of the joinery, with specific cost bands typical in the region. A budget custom fit-out, often using painted MDF or melamine, generally falls between $8,000 and $14,000. This usually covers 10 to 15 carcasses with standard hardware. Mid-range projects, costing between $14,000 and $22,000, typically upgrade to two-pack polyurethane or timber veneer doors, integrated pantries and 40mm stone benchtops. For those seeking a luxury finish, expenses can exceed $35,000. This tier includes solid timber species like blackbutt or Victorian ash, dovetail joinery and concealed fittings. Labour remains the largest cost driver, accounting for 40 to 55 per cent of the total price. Island benchtops and full-height overhead cabinetry significantly add to the final tally, as these elements require more material and precise installation compared to standard configurations.

Regulatory Compliance in Victoria

Regulatory compliance is non-negotiable for structural and installation work. The Victorian Building Authority requires that any domestic building work valued over $10,000 be performed by a registered building practitioner. Homeowners must verify these credentials through the official VBA registry before signing a contract. Furthermore, legislation mandates Domestic Building Insurance for projects exceeding $16,000, providing financial protection if the contractor fails to complete the work. Specific trades also face restrictions; plumbing tasks such as sink relocation must be handled by VBA-registered plumbers, while electrical work for appliances and lighting requires a license from Energy Safe Victoria. While standalone cabinet installation rarely triggers a building permit, moving plumbing or altering structural walls changes the classification. Residents in the City of Greater Geelong should also check for heritage overlays that might impose additional restrictions on alterations.

Project Timeline and Phases

The timeline from concept to completion involves several distinct stages that require careful coordination. It begins with a site measure where the joiner records ceiling height variations and the location of services. Designers then produce 3D visualisations to ensure the layout meets the homeowner's needs before fabrication begins. The manufacturing process usually takes two to four weeks. Installation follows, but it must be carefully sequenced. Cabinets are fixed in place first to establish the layout for subsequent trades. Stone benchtops require a separate templating visit once cabinets are installed, with a turnaround of typically five to seven days. During this window, plumbers and electricians complete rough-ins for sinks and power points. The final fit-off involves attaching handles, kickboards and pelmets, transforming the raw components into a functional kitchen space.

  1. Site Measure and Brief. The joiner measures the space in full, noting ceiling height variations, wall plumb and service locations.
  2. Design and Selection. Review 3D visualisations and select board species, door profiles, hardware systems and benchtop materials.
  3. Fabrication. Carcasses and doors are cut, edged and assembled in the workshop over a period of two to four weeks.
  4. Installation and Fit-off. Cabinets are installed, followed by benchtop templating. Plumbing and electrical are coordinated before final handles and kickboards are fitted.

Common questions

Do I need a building permit for new cabinets? Generally, no. A standalone cabinet installation in the same position does not usually require a permit. However, moving the sink, altering structural walls or adding extraction ducting may trigger a building permit requirement.

How long does the fabrication process take? The lead time for custom cabinetry is typically around six weeks. This includes the time for design finalisation, material ordering, cutting, edging and assembly in the workshop prior to installation.
