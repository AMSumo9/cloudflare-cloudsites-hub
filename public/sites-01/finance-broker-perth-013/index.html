Questions Before Choosing A Broker

A useful broker call turns a borrowing goal into a shortlist of realistic options. Before the first call, write down the property purpose, preferred repayment style, deposit position, existing debts, income type and whether the loan is for purchase, refinance, construction, vehicle, equipment or business use.

ASIC Moneysmart gives consumer guidance on comparing home loans by looking beyond the headline interest rate to fees, features and whether the loan suits the borrower. In a broker conversation, ask what lenders were considered, why a suggested loan fits, what fees apply and what could change before approval.

Ask for trade offs in plain language. An offset account may suit a borrower who keeps cash available, while a basic variable loan may suit someone who needs fewer features. A fixed rate can support repayment certainty, but ask about break costs, redraw limits and what happens when the fixed period ends.
