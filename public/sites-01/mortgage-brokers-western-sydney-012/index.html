Fees, LMI And Serviceability

The published guide says most standard home loans do not involve a direct broker fee because brokers are typically paid by the lender once the loan settles. It also says some complex scenarios, including some commercial or low-doc loans, may involve a fee, and that any fee should be disclosed in writing up front.

Deposit and LMI questions need the same clarity. The guide says many lenders require at least 5% of the purchase price as genuine savings, and that borrowing more than 80% of the property value usually triggers Lenders Mortgage Insurance. It describes LMI as a one-off premium that protects the lender, not the borrower, and says it is usually capitalised into the loan.

Borrowing power also needs review. The guide says lenders consider income, existing debts, living expenses and serviceability, and may compare declared expenses with the Household Expenditure Measure. It also says repayments are tested at the actual rate plus the APRA 3% prudential buffer. ASIC Moneysmart provides general public guidance on home loans for borrowers wanting background reading.

Comparing Broker Recommendations

A recommendation should show the trade-offs, not just a lender name. The published guide says borrowers can compare loans and features from a wide panel of lenders, with help from application through to settlement. Ask to see the requested loan amount, deposit position, LMI treatment, interest type, offset option, fees and settlement support.

Bring recent payslips or business income evidence, current loan statements, living expense notes, savings history and the purchase or refinance goal. Investors should add rental and portfolio details. Construction borrowers should add the contract and progress-payment material when available. Refinancers should ask whether the restructure still improves the position after switching costs and changed loan features.

Before proceeding, ask for the comparison, broker remuneration and any direct fee in writing, then read the credit proposal documents before signing.

  1. Define the loan purpose. Separate purchase, refinance, investment, construction, bridging and self-employed needs before asking for loan options.
  2. Prepare evidence. Gather income, expense, debt, savings and property details so serviceability can be checked properly.
  3. Ask about payment. Request written disclosure of broker remuneration and any direct fee before proceeding.
  4. Compare features. Review rate type, offset access, LMI treatment, fees and settlement support together.

Common questions

Do brokers usually charge borrowers directly? The published guide says most standard home loans have no direct broker fee because the broker is typically paid by the lender after settlement. It also says complex scenarios may involve a fee, which should be disclosed in writing up front.

When does LMI usually become part of the discussion? The published guide says LMI usually applies when borrowing more than 80% of the property value. It describes LMI as insurance for the lender, not personal protection for the borrower.

What should a Western Sydney borrower prepare before a review? Prepare the loan goal, suburb or postcode, income evidence, current debts, living expenses and any current loan details. Self-employed and construction borrowers should expect extra document questions.

Is a borrowing calculator enough to rely on? No. The published guide says its calculator gives an estimate only, while a broker can review the borrower's full situation before any commitment.
