What to check before comparing lenders

A useful lender comparison starts with inputs that can be verified. ASIC Moneysmart's home loan guidance encourages borrowers to understand loan costs and repayment commitments, which fits the source page's focus on checking repayments alongside deposit, strata and moving costs.

Before asking for a shortlist, prepare a plain summary of the purchase goal, suburb or postcode, rough price range, deposit position, income type and the decision date. For an existing loan, add current rate type, fixed-rate expiry if relevant, remaining balance, repayment amount and whether equity access or debt consolidation is part of the question.

Investors should be ready to discuss rental assumptions and servicing pressure rather than treating investment property lending as the same conversation as an owner-occupied purchase. The source page also separates commercial loans, equipment finance and business borrowing as adjacent needs, so borrowers should be clear when the loan is for a home, an investment, a business asset or mixed goals.

Local questions that are worth asking

Parramatta borrowers do not need a long suburb list to make the conversation local. The practical differences come from property type, buyer urgency and evidence. An apartment purchase in a dense pocket may raise strata and repayment questions earlier, while a family-home search may make pre-approval range and offer timing more important.

Useful questions include:

  • Does the lender view this property type differently, and what documents should be ready before an offer?
  • If using a low deposit, what are the LMI, guarantor and repayment trade-offs?
  • For a first-home purchase, which NSW grant or duty settings need checking before the shortlist tightens?
  • For a refinance, what costs and equity goals should be tested before leaving the current lender?
  • For self-employed income, which tax, BAS or entity details could change lender choice?

The strongest outcome is a cleaner decision file, not a rushed application built around one advertised rate.

How to read the comparison

The comparison should connect the borrower scenario to the lender's appetite. A rate can look attractive while the structure is wrong for deposit size, income evidence, pre-approval timing or future investment plans. A broader review can also show when staying put is better than refinancing immediately.

The source page presents tools and resources before enquiry, including a borrowing-power calculator and planning checklist. Use those as a preparation step, then treat the review as a pressure test of the assumptions behind the numbers. If a grant, duty setting or regulator context is involved, confirm the live rule before committing to a contract or lender pathway.

Mortgage and Finance Association of Australia membership is not stated on the source page, so this guide does not imply it. The more grounded check is whether the broker explains scope, lender comparison, fees if any, documents required and how recommendations are made.

  1. Define the loan path. Choose whether the immediate issue is first purchase, low deposit, pre-approval, refinance, investment or self-employed income.
  2. Collect the evidence. Prepare income documents, deposit position, current loan details if refinancing, and the suburb or postcode being considered.
  3. Test the assumptions. Check repayments, strata or moving costs, NSW scheme questions and lender policy before relying on one option.
  4. Compare the lender fit. Ask which lenders suit the scenario and why, including trade-offs around LMI, guarantors, equity use or document quality.
  5. Decide the next step. Move to pre-approval, refinance analysis or a paused search only after the key blocker has been answered.
