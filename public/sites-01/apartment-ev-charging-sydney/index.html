Apartment Ev Charging Sydney Explained

Ev Charger Installation Sydney identifies apartment and strata charging as a separate planning problem from a detached-home installation. Begin by confirming whether the vehicle will use a basement bay, where the charger could be mounted and who controls the relevant electrical infrastructure.

Owners corporation approval is usually part of the process. A proposal should explain the proposed charger position, the route for wiring, how the building supply will be protected from excessive demand and how the electricity used by each owner will be measured or billed. These questions are more useful to a strata committee than a charger model chosen in isolation.

  • Confirm the parking bay and wall or mounting surface.
  • Ask which switchboard or consumer mains section would supply the charger.
  • Request the building's preferred process for approval and electricity billing.

Match charging speed to the car and supply

A single-phase 7 kW wall charger suits most cars and homes, according to the published Sydney guidance. A three-phase 22 kW installation can be appropriate where the property has that supply and the vehicle can accept the higher rate, but the larger number alone does not make it the better apartment choice.

For an apartment, ask for the car's maximum AC charging rate, the available supply, the cable route and any proposed load-management arrangement. A Tesla Wall Connector is one option described for Sydney properties, while smart chargers can add scheduling and load balancing. The useful comparison is the equipment's actual fit with the vehicle and building, not the headline charging speed.

Read the quote through its cost signals

A written quote should make the physical work visible. Cable length, wall penetrations, mounting position and switchboard condition are identified as factors that drive installation cost. In a basement, the route from the relevant electrical board to the parking bay may be more important than the charger casing itself.

Compare quotes line by line and ask whether each one states the charger model, circuit arrangement, cable route, mounting method, testing, compliance paperwork and any switchboard work. If the proposal depends on information that has not been inspected, ask what must be confirmed before the price is final. Do not treat a low headline figure as comparable when one quote leaves these items open.

A solar-aware charger may be relevant to a property with rooftop solar, CT clamps or battery storage, but apartment owners should first establish whether the proposed system can use that generation and who controls it.

Understand NSW electrical and approval steps

The published guidance says council approval is usually not needed when an installation fits exempt development rules. If it does not, a development application or similar pathway may apply. Work that touches a footpath or road reserve is separate and requires agreement from the roads authority.

Fixed charger wiring is electrical work in NSW and needs an appropriately licensed electrician. There is no separate EV charger licence. After testing, the electrician must lodge a Certificate of Compliance for Electrical Work through BCNSW eCert within seven days, and the owner should ask for a copy.

These steps do not replace strata approval. Keep the owners corporation decision, the final quote, the charger information and the compliance record together so a future owner or committee can understand the installation.

Who this guide applies to

This guide is for Sydney apartment and strata residents who have an EV, have one on order or are assessing whether a home charging arrangement will work in a shared car park. It is most useful before a quote is requested, when the parking bay, building approval route and electrical supply are still being checked.

A townhouse or detached home may have a simpler approval path, but the charger still needs a dedicated circuit, a suitable parking position and an installation matched to the vehicle and supply.

Common questions

Can an EV charger be installed in a Sydney apartment car park? Usually, but owners corporation approval is generally needed under NSW strata rules. The building may also need a load-management plan and a fair way to bill each owner for the electricity used.

Is a 22 kW charger automatically better than a 7 kW charger? No. A 7 kW single-phase charger suits most cars and homes. A 22 kW three-phase option only helps when the vehicle can accept that charging rate and the property has suitable supply.

Can an apartment owner install fixed charger wiring personally? No. Fixed charger wiring is electrical work in NSW and needs an appropriately licensed electrician. The installation must be tested, and the electrician must lodge the compliance certificate through BCNSW eCert within seven days of testing.

What should an apartment charging quote include? It should make the charger, circuit, cable length and route, wall penetrations, mounting position, switchboard condition, testing and compliance paperwork clear. Ask what remains subject to site inspection or strata approval.
