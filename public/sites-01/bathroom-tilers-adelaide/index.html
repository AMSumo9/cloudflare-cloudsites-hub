Bathroom Tilers Adelaide Explained

Adelaide Tiling & Waterproofing frames bathroom work around the layers below the finished surface: the existing base, water exposure, fixtures, drainage and tile format. That is a useful starting point because the neatness of the grout line is only one part of the job. A quote that jumps straight to a square metre rate can miss the decisions that affect preparation, waterproofing and sequencing.

A stronger enquiry describes what is changing and what is staying. Note whether the room is a full bathroom, ensuite, laundry edge or shower area, whether moisture is already visible, and whether previous repairs have been attempted. Photos and approximate dimensions can help a provider decide what can be priced from supplied information and what must be inspected before a scope is reliable.

  • List the surfaces to be tiled, including walls, floors, niches and adjoining transitions.
  • Identify wastes, thresholds, fixture positions and cabinetry lines before ordering finishes.
  • Ask whether screed to falls, membrane work, trims, movement joints or substrate preparation are included or excluded.

Where waterproofing changes the decision

The source page makes a clear distinction between tiles as finishes and the waterproof layer behind them. It states that tiles and grout do not make a shower waterproof. For a bathroom renovation, that means the tiler discussion should include membrane boundaries, penetrations, thresholds, cure sequencing and how the handover from waterproofing to tiling will be recorded.

For a leak or failed shower finish, the scope question is different. Regrouting may suit a maintenance issue, but the page cautions against treating it as a universal leak repair. A diagnosis-led scope should separate sealant, plumbing, substrate movement and membrane assumptions before the work is reduced to a surface repair. This is where written inclusions matter more than verbal confidence.

Ask the provider to state the product system used, the preparation required by that system and the point at which tiling can begin. The page does not give a universal curing period, and that is appropriate because cure timing depends on the membrane system, film thickness and site conditions.

  • For newer slabs, ask how movement joints and adjoining floor heights will be handled.

Comparing written scopes

A useful tiling quote identifies preparation, tile format, layout, trims, grout, movement joints, access and waste. If waterproofing or screeding is required, the source page says those items should be separately described. That separation helps prevent a quote for a finished surface being mistaken for a complete wet-area scope.

Licensing should also be checked before accepting a quote. The source page notes that waterproofing and tiling may sit within one coordinated scope where the contractor holds the appropriate South Australian authorisation for the work involved. Rather than assuming one trade covers every task, ask for the licence scope and for any subcontracted work to be identified in writing.

Cost comparison is most useful when competing scopes describe the same room, the same assumed substrate, the same waterproofing boundaries and the same finish details. If one scope includes demolition, screed correction, waterproofing and waste while another only lists tiling labour, the totals are not comparable.

Before work starts

Before demolition, record the existing room, any suspected moisture, access limits, fixture changes and any hazardous-material questions that need to be resolved by the responsible party. The source page uses this stage to make hidden assumptions visible before the room is stripped and variations become harder to control.

Before waterproofing, confirm the substrate, falls, wastes, penetrations, thresholds and membrane system with the provider. Before tiling, set out tile modules, niches, trims, movement joints, cabinetry lines and adjoining floor transitions together. These are practical checkpoints because they connect the design decision to the build sequence.

  1. Send a concise room brief with photos, approximate dimensions and the suburb or postcode.
  2. Ask what must be inspected before the provider can stand behind the scope.
  3. Request written inclusions, exclusions and licensed work details before comparing prices.
  1. Describe the condition. Note the room, age, visible symptoms, previous repairs, access limits and what fixtures may move.
  2. Separate the layers. Ask the provider to distinguish plumbing, substrate, waterproofing, screed and tile finish assumptions.
  3. Compare like for like. Check preparation, inclusions, exclusions, waste, waterproofing, screeding and licensed work details before comparing totals.
  4. Confirm handover points. Record when demolition, waterproofing, curing and tiling each become the responsibility of the next trade or stage.

Common questions

Do tiles and grout waterproof a shower? No. The source page states that tiles and grout are finishes, while the compliant membrane and correctly treated junctions form the waterproof layer behind them.

Can the same contractor handle waterproofing and tiling? They may be delivered within one coordinated scope where the contractor holds the appropriate South Australian authorisation for the work involved. Check the licence scope before accepting a quote.

How long should waterproofing cure before tiling? The source page does not give a universal period. It says cure time depends on the membrane system, film thickness and site conditions, and the installer should follow the product specification.

What should be included in an Adelaide tiling quote? A useful quote identifies preparation, tile format, layout, trims, grout, movement joints, access and waste. Waterproofing or screeding should be separately described where required.
