First Home Buyer Loans Brisbane Costs Explained

Buying a first home in Brisbane requires funds beyond the deposit. The most significant upfront cost is usually stamp duty, a state tax on property transfers. Queensland offers concessions or full waivers for eligible first home buyers, but these depend on the property value. If the property exceeds the threshold, duty applies to the full amount or the portion above the cap.

Conveyancing fees cover the legal work required to transfer ownership. These fees vary but typically range between one and two thousand dollars. Lenders also charge establishment or application fees, which cover the cost of setting up the loan. Some lenders waive these fees to attract new customers, so checking fee schedules is important when reviewing first home buyer loans brisbane comparison data.

Lenders Mortgage Insurance Explained

Lenders mortgage insurance (LMI) is a major cost for buyers with a smaller deposit. If your deposit is less than 20 percent of the property value, the lender will likely require LMI. This insurance protects the lender if you default on the loan, not the borrower. The premium is a one-off payment that can be added to the loan amount or paid upfront.

The cost of LMI depends on the loan size and the loan-to-value ratio (LVR). It can run into tens of thousands of dollars. However, the federal Home Guarantee Scheme allows eligible first home buyers to purchase with a 5 percent deposit without paying LMI. The government acts as the guarantor, significantly reducing the upfront barrier to entry for those who meet the income and property price caps.

Ongoing Loan Charges and Interest

The ongoing cost of a loan is determined by the interest rate and the principal balance. Fixed rates provide certainty for a set period, while variable rates fluctuate with the market. The comparison rate is a useful tool as it includes the interest rate plus most fees and charges, giving a true cost of the loan.

Borrowers should also account for annual package fees or monthly account keeping fees. Some loans offer offset accounts or redraw facilities, which can reduce interest paid but may come with higher fees. Additionally, factor in council rates, water rates and building insurance, as these are ongoing home ownership costs that lenders consider when assessing your serviceability.

Reducing Your Total Expense

Minimising the total cost involves strategic planning. Saving a deposit of 20 percent avoids LMI entirely. For those unable to reach this threshold, utilising the First Home Guarantee Scheme or a family guarantor loan can help avoid LMI. Family guarantors use equity in their own property as security, reducing the risk for the lender.

Common questions

What is the First Home Owner Grant worth? The grant amount and property value cap change periodically, so it is essential to check the Queensland Revenue Office for current figures. It is a one-off payment for eligible new homes.

Can I borrow the stamp duty costs? Some lenders allow you to capitalise stamp duty and LMI into the loan amount. While this preserves your cash savings, it increases the total principal and the interest you pay over time.

Do I need a solicitor? Yes, conveyancing is a critical part of the process. A solicitor or conveyancer handles the legal transfer of title, reviews contracts and ensures all conditions are met for settlement.
