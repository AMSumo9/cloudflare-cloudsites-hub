Ev Charger Installation Melbourne Explained

Ev Charger Installation Melbourne explains that a home unit is normally mounted in a garage, carport or driveway and wired on its own circuit. Before requesting a quote, note where the car sits overnight and estimate the distance to the switchboard. Cable length and the wall or mounting surface can change the work required, even when two households choose the same charger.

The parking position also affects the route into the house. A garage may offer a straightforward wall installation, while an open driveway can require more planning around the cable path and mounting point. Record whether the charger will be inside or outside, whether the vehicle can park consistently in that position, and whether the charger has already been purchased.

  • Identify the usual parking spot.
  • Estimate the route from that spot to the meter box or switchboard.
  • Note the wall type, charger model and any access limits.

Read the switchboard before choosing power

Older Melbourne homes may have switchboards that were not designed for overnight vehicle charging. A quote should therefore show whether the board needs an upgrade, a load assessment, load management or added protection. Newer homes may already have a suitable dedicated circuit, but that should be confirmed rather than assumed from the age of the property.

Supply type is another decision point. A single-phase installation may suit the vehicle and household demand, while a three-phase unit can deliver 11 to 22kW. The car's onboard charger may not use all of that capacity, so paying for a faster unit only makes sense when the vehicle, supply and intended charging pattern justify it.

Homeowners can look at the main switch and breakers, note whether the supply appears single or three-phase, and record whether solar panels or a battery are connected. That information gives an electrician a clearer starting point, but it does not replace an assessment of the installation.

Compare charger choices on the same terms

Charger selection should follow the vehicle and the home rather than the headline power rating. A universal Type 2 unit may suit a car requiring a general home charging option. A Tesla Wall Connector is relevant when the vehicle and chosen equipment are compatible. A solar-aware charger can use surplus rooftop generation and cheaper tariff windows, subject to the way the existing inverter and household system operate.

Keep quotes comparable by asking each electrician to identify the charger model, cable route, mounting method, switchboard work, protection and certification. A lower total can reflect a shorter route or a narrower scope, not necessarily better value.

Allow for apartments and shared parking

A unit, townhouse or apartment with a shared car park introduces decisions that do not arise in the same way for a detached home. The installation may need owners corporation approval, a clear metering arrangement and a plan for load sharing. Those questions should be answered before equipment is ordered or an installation date is discussed.

Ask who controls the parking space, where the electrical supply comes from, how usage will be measured and whether other residents could be affected by the proposed circuit. A private charger can still depend on common property access, building rules or shared infrastructure. If approval is required, keep the written decision with the quote and equipment records.

Check Victorian paperwork before the job closes

In Victoria, the electrician or Registered Electrical Contractor completing the work must issue a Certificate of Electrical Safety and give the homeowner a copy. Ask when the certificate will be supplied, what work it covers and whether any prescribed work requires independent inspection. Keeping this document with the charger model, invoice and quote creates a useful record for the property.

Australian wiring rules, including AS/NZS 3000, govern the charger circuit's protection, cabling and installation methods. A homeowner does not need to interpret every technical clause, but should ask the electrician to explain the selected circuit, protection and isolation arrangements in plain language.

Before accepting the completed job, check that the charger is mounted as agreed, the cable route is clear, the switchboard work shown in the quote has been addressed and the certificate has been provided. If the scope changed, ask for the revised details in writing.

  1. Picture where the car parks. Note whether the vehicle uses a garage, carport or open driveway and estimate the distance to the switchboard.
  2. Check the supply. Record the apparent switchboard age, main switch, breakers, single or three-phase supply, and any solar or battery connection.
  3. Choose the equipment. Decide whether to discuss a universal Type 2 unit, Tesla Wall Connector, solar-aware charger or three-phase option.
  4. Request a comparable quote. Provide the suburb, parking position, charger details and property information, then ask for the cable, switchboard and certification scope in writing.

Common questions

What usually changes the installation price? Cable run length, mounting location, charger type and switchboard condition are the main variables described by the local guide. Compare those items line by line rather than comparing totals alone.

Can a three-phase charger deliver its full output to every car? No assumption should be made. Three-phase chargers can deliver 11 to 22kW, but the vehicle's onboard charger may not accept all of that capacity.

What paperwork should a Victorian homeowner receive? The electrician or Registered Electrical Contractor must issue a Certificate of Electrical Safety and provide a copy. Ask about any independent inspection that applies to prescribed work.

What should apartment owners ask first? Ask about owners corporation approval, metering, load sharing, parking rights and access to shared electrical infrastructure before ordering equipment.
