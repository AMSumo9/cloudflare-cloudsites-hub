Ev Charger Installation Melbourne Explained

The Ev Charger Installation Melbourne guide recommends starting with the vehicle's actual parking position. A garage, carport and open driveway can each change the mounting method, cable route and amount of work required. Measure or estimate the distance back to the switchboard, and note the wall or surface where the charger could be fixed.

The route is more useful than a simple charger price when comparing proposals. A short run to a nearby wall may be straightforward, while a longer route across a different part of the property can require more cabling and different installation methods. Include whether the charger has already been purchased, and keep its model name available for the electrician.

  • Record the parking location and approximate cable distance.
  • Note whether the charger will be mounted on a suitable wall or another structure.
  • Identify whether the car is already owned or still on order.
  • Ask each quote to show cable, mounting and switchboard work separately.

Check the switchboard before choosing speed

Older Melbourne homes may have switchboards that were not designed around overnight vehicle charging. The relevant question is not simply whether a charger can be attached, but whether the existing board and supply can carry the additional circuit with suitable protection. A load assessment, switchboard upgrade or load-management arrangement may be considered after the electrician reviews the installation.

Newer estate homes may already have a dedicated circuit or a supply suited to a three-phase unit. Three-phase chargers are described in the guide as delivering 11 to 22kW, but the vehicle's onboard charger can limit how much power it actually uses. Paying for a higher-rated unit therefore makes sense only after matching the charger to both the supply and the car.

Ask for the proposed circuit arrangement, the protection included, and any work needed before installation. If the quote assumes an upgrade, request the reason in plain language and ask whether load management is an alternative.

Match the charger to the household energy setup

A universal Type 2 charger, a Tesla Wall Connector and a solar-aware unit present different selection questions. The right choice depends on the vehicle, the equipment already bought and how the household wants to charge. A Tesla Wall Connector can be installed for a unit already purchased, while a universal option may suit a household that wants broader vehicle compatibility.

Solar and battery homes need an additional conversation about the existing inverter, surplus daytime generation and tariff windows. A solar-aware charger can use excess rooftop generation, but the electrician still needs to understand how that equipment fits with the home's current electrical setup. Ask whether the proposed charger can work with the existing system and what charging behaviour it is designed to support.

Do not treat the highest charging figure as the only measure. Vehicle compatibility, supply capacity, cable route and the household's preferred charging times all belong in the comparison.

Apartments, townhouses and shared parking

Shared parking changes the project from a private wall installation into a property-management question as well. Apartments, units and townhouses may involve owners corporation approval, separate metering and decisions about load sharing. These issues should be raised before equipment is ordered, because the parking bay, switchboard and electricity account may not all belong to the same dwelling.

Ask who controls the proposed mounting surface, where the circuit would begin, how electricity use would be recorded and whether other residents could later need access to shared capacity. A clear site description should include the parking bay, the nearest switchboard, whether the space is enclosed, and any known owners corporation process.

For a townhouse with private parking, the arrangement may be closer to a detached-home installation, but the actual supply and property rules still need checking. The separate guide to planning a Melbourne home charger installation is useful when comparing these site questions with a standard residential setup.

Paperwork and quote checks in Victoria

In Victoria, the electrician or Registered Electrical Contractor completing the work must issue a Certificate of Electrical Safety and provide a copy. Keep that document with the charger information and any switchboard records. The guide also notes that AS/NZS 3000 sets wiring requirements for protection, cabling and installation methods, and that some prescribed work requires an independent Licensed Electrical Inspector.

Before accepting a quote, check whether it identifies the charger model, circuit work, cable route, mounting position, switchboard assumptions and certification. Ask what happens if the board needs an upgrade after inspection, and whether the quoted work includes the final paperwork. These questions make two otherwise similar proposals easier to compare.

A useful enquiry includes the suburb, property type, parking location, charger details if known, approximate distance to the switchboard, and whether solar or a battery is installed. That information gives the electrician a more realistic starting point than a charger model alone.

  1. Map the parking spot. Record whether the vehicle sits in a garage, carport or open driveway and estimate the distance to the switchboard.
  2. Inspect the supply. Note whether the home appears to have single-phase or three-phase supply, and whether solar or a battery is connected.
  3. Choose a direction. Identify a universal Type 2 charger, Tesla Wall Connector, solar-aware unit or a request for advice.
  4. Request comparable quotes. Send the property and charger details, then ask each proposal to identify cable, mounting, switchboard and certification items.

Common questions

What usually changes the cost of a home charger installation? The main variables are cable-run length, mounting location, charger type and switchboard condition. Older homes may need additional electrical work, while some newer homes may already suit a dedicated circuit.

Will an 11 to 22kW charger always charge a car at that speed? No. The guide explains that a vehicle's onboard charger may accept less than the three-phase unit can deliver. The charger should be matched to both the home's supply and the car's charging limit.

What paperwork should a Victorian homeowner receive? The electrician or Registered Electrical Contractor should issue a Certificate of Electrical Safety and provide a copy. AS/NZS 3000 requirements and any applicable inspection process should also be discussed.

What should apartment owners check first? Check the parking arrangement, owners corporation approval, metering and load-sharing requirements before ordering equipment. Shared infrastructure can affect both the route and the way charging electricity is allocated.
