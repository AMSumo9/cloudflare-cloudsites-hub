Kitchen Renovation Melbourne South Explained

Melbourne South Kitchen Renovation describes the common starting points as failing drawers, doors that will not stay shut, limited bench space and layouts that no longer suit the household. Those symptoms should become specific design questions: where is preparation happening, which items need daily access, and whether storage should be concentrated in drawers, tall pantry units or a butler's pantry?

The local guide covers Melbourne's south east and outer east, including Wantirna South, Wantirna, Vermont South, Blackburn South, Glen Waverley and Scoresby. The relevant difference is not the suburb list itself, but the variation in house age, room shape and block size. A kitchen fitted in the 1980s or 1990s may need a different storage plan from a compact room in a newer or altered layout.

Measure the room, note door swings and windows, and record anything nearby that could affect access. If a laundry or vanity is being considered at the same time, include it in the initial brief so the trades and sequence can be discussed together.

Choose the level of joinery before comparing quotes

Flat pack cabinets can suit a straightforward replacement where the room dimensions and layout are settled. Custom cabinetry becomes more relevant when the room needs tall pantry storage, a butler's pantry, unusual dimensions or joinery built around the way the household cooks. Neither option is automatically the better result. The useful comparison is how much storage, fit and finish the room requires, and what the budget can carry.

Benchtop selection also changes the planning conversation. Natural stone needs to be considered alongside the sink, cooktop and splashback, rather than treated as a final surface choice. A quote should identify cabinets, benchtops, handles, appliances if included, demolition, disposal and each trade allowance.

Use the written scope to separate confirmed selections from allowances. In an older home, removing cabinets can reveal walls or pipework that could not be inspected properly beforehand. An allowance does not remove that uncertainty, but it makes the assumption visible before work starts.

Understand the order of work

A coordinated renovation usually follows a practical sequence. Existing cabinets are removed, then plumbing and electrical rough-in are completed before walls close up. Cabinet installation follows, with benchtops and the remaining fittings coordinated around the room. Tiling and painting finish the visible surfaces.

  1. Confirm the layout, selections, scope and allowances.
  2. Remove the old kitchen and inspect the exposed room.
  3. Complete required plumbing and electrical work.
  4. Install cabinetry, benchtops and connected fittings.
  5. Finish tiling and painting, then check doors, drawers and surfaces.

The exact schedule depends on the scope. Keeping the existing layout can finish sooner than moving plumbing or adding walls, while custom cabinets add lead time before installation. Ask for a written schedule showing the expected period without a working kitchen and the decisions needed before each trade arrives.

Check responsibility, certificates and warranties

Where cabinetry, plumbing, electrical work and tiling are combined, the quote should name the registered building practitioner or building company responsible for running the job. The published guidance says regulated plumbing and electrical work in Victoria must be completed by licensed trades who issue certificates. Ask which person or company is responsible for each part and when the relevant certificate will be supplied.

Keep product and workmanship responsibilities separate. Manufacturer warranties may apply to cabinets, benchtops and appliances, while the contractor's workmanship warranty concerns installation. Australian Consumer Law guarantees also apply to fixed items such as stoves, sinks, tapware and extractor fans once they are permanently part of the building. Keep the final selections, invoices, certificates and warranty documents together.

Who this guide applies to

This guide is for homeowners in Melbourne's south east and outer east who are assessing a full replacement, a compact kitchen redesign or a staged improvement. It is especially relevant when drawers, doors, bench space or an older layout are driving the project, and when the work may include plumbing, electrical changes, cabinetry, stone, tiling or painting.

It is less useful as a final quote because the room must be measured and inspected before a reliable scope can be agreed. Before requesting a project review, prepare the suburb or postcode, access information, the parts that are failing, preferred storage, any material choices and whether the layout is staying in place.

  1. Record the problem. Write down the drawers, doors, bench areas and storage points that no longer work.
  2. Map the room. Measure the kitchen and note windows, access, plumbing points and nearby rooms affected by the work.
  3. Set the scope. Choose the layout direction and list cabinets, benchtops, appliances, tiling, painting and trade work.
  4. Compare written quotes. Check inclusions, allowances, sequencing, responsibility for licensed trades and the expected kitchen outage.
  5. Keep the documents. Retain the agreed scope, selections, certificates, invoices and product or workmanship warranty information.
