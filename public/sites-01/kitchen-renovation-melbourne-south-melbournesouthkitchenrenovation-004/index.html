Kitchen Renovation Melbourne South Explained

Melbourne South Kitchen Renovation describes the starting point as a practical one: doors that will not stay shut, insufficient bench space, or an older kitchen that no longer suits the household. Those problems should shape the brief before finishes are selected.

In the south east and outer east, homes and block sizes vary. Wantirna South, Wantirna, Vermont South, Blackburn South, Glen Waverley and Scoresby may contain different room proportions and different renovation constraints. A useful brief records how the kitchen is used, where storage is failing, whether the existing sink or cooktop should move, and whether a laundry or vanity should be considered in the same project.

A room-by-room assessment is more useful than choosing cabinets from a catalogue. It helps determine whether the priority is more drawers, a tall pantry wall, additional bench space, a butler's pantry, or a compact layout that makes better use of limited floor area.

Choose the scope before comparing finishes

The main cost signals are kitchen size, materials and service changes. Flat pack cabinets generally sit at the lower end of the available options, while custom joinery with natural stone sits at the higher end. That comparison is only meaningful when both quotes describe the same work.

Ask for each proposal to separate cabinets, benchtops, appliances if included, plumbing, electrical work, tiling, painting and removal of the old kitchen. The written scope should also identify allowances for hidden issues in an older home. Once cabinets are removed, walls and pipework become visible, but a scope cannot remove every unknown.

  • Retain the existing layout if keeping the project simpler is important.
  • Move plumbing or electrical positions only when the improvement justifies added coordination.
  • Compare flat pack and custom joinery by storage, room fit and installation detail, not by cabinet price alone.
  • Confirm whether natural stone includes the sink, cooktop and splashback planning required by the layout.

Sequence the trades and protect the schedule

A full project moves through a sequence rather than a single installation. Demolition exposes the room, plumbing and electrical rough-in occur before walls close up, cabinets are installed, and tiling and painting finish the space. The cabinet maker, plumber, electrician and tiler therefore need to be booked in a sensible order.

A kitchen that keeps its existing layout can finish sooner than one with moved plumbing or new walls. Custom cabinets add lead time before installation. The useful question is not simply how many days the work may take, but how many days the household will be without a working kitchen. Ask for a written schedule and identify which decisions must be final before ordering.

Who this guide applies to

This guide suits homeowners planning a compact update, a full replacement of a 20 to 30 year old kitchen, or a larger project that combines cabinetry with plumbing, electrical work and tiling. It is also relevant when a laundry or vanity may be included in the same job.

It is less useful as a decision shortcut for someone who has not yet worked out the basic problem. Before requesting a quote, note what currently fails, measure the room, photograph access points, and record nearby features that could affect the job. Include whether the property has an older layout, whether service positions may change, and whether the preference is flat pack, custom joinery or natural stone.

For regulated work in Victoria, the quote should name the people or company responsible for each part. The published guidance says plumbing and electrical work must be completed by licensed trades who issue certificates, while a job combining cabinetry, plumbing, electrical work and tiling should identify the registered building practitioner or building company running it.

Check the quote, warranty and handover

A strong quote gives the household a firm basis for comparison before anything is removed. Check that the scope and price for fittings and trades are written down, that allowances for hidden issues are visible, and that the schedule explains the order of work. Ask who coordinates the trades and who is responsible for certificates.

Warranty questions should be divided by product and installation. Manufacturer warranties may apply to cabinets, benchtops and appliances, while a contractor's workmanship warranty relates to installation. Australian Consumer Law guarantees also apply to fixed items such as stoves, sinks, tapware and extractor fans once they become permanently part of the building.

At handover, check doors and drawers, benchtop joins, splashback finish, sink and tap connections, appliance positions, paint and tile work. Keep the final scope, certificates, warranty information and any agreed changes together so later questions can be traced to the documented job.

  1. Describe the failure. Record what no longer works, such as poor storage, insufficient bench space or doors that do not stay shut.
  2. Set the room brief. Note how the household cooks, whether the layout or service positions may change, and whether another room is included.
  3. Request an itemised scope. Ask for cabinets, benchtops, trades, removal, allowances and exclusions to be written down.
  4. Confirm trade order. Check when plumbing, electrical rough-in, cabinetry, tiling and painting occur and who coordinates them.
  5. Review handover documents. Collect certificates, warranties, the final scope and agreed changes before treating the project as complete.
