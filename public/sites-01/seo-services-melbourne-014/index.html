The SEO Company Melbourne presents the audit as the first serious filter: a written review of how Google can read the site, whether pages are indexed, page experience, on-page fixes, internal linking, backlinks and the top three competitor gaps for the main keyword.

That matters because the audit turns a vague growth conversation into a list of work. A retailer with slow category pages, a local service business missing suburb relevance and a consultant with thin service pages should not receive the same plan. Ask which issues are blockers, which are optional improvements, and which tasks belong in the first month.

  • Request the audit in writing before discussing a monthly retainer.

  • Keep the top three competitor gaps attached to the proposal, not buried in a slide deck.

Who This Applies To

This guide best suits Melbourne and Greater Victoria businesses that already have a website and want a clearer way to assess organic search work. The source page names local businesses, online stores and small businesses competing in local or niche markets, so the advice is most relevant where search visibility supports enquiries or sales.

It is less useful for a brand that only wants one-off copywriting, social media posting or a guaranteed ranking claim. The published page explicitly says no reputable SEO specialist promises first-page rankings because Google controls the algorithm. That makes the better question measurable work: what pages will be improved, what technical issues will be fixed, what content will be created, and what links or mentions will be earned.

  • Local service business: check Maps, directory listings, reviews and suburb pages.
  • Online store: check category, product and intent-matched content plans.
  • Established site: check crawl, indexation and internal links before expanding content.

Reporting That Shows Work, Not Noise

The published positioning favours monthly reporting tied to actual work completed rather than traffic graphs alone. That distinction is valuable because traffic can move for reasons outside the campaign, including seasonality, branded searches or paid activity. A useful monthly report should connect actions to evidence.

Ask for the completed technical fixes, pages updated, keywords mapped, links earned, content published and next priorities. Search engine optimisation is not only a ranking exercise. It combines keyword research, on-page changes, technical accessibility for crawlers, content quality, internal linking and backlinks. A report that only lists positions can miss whether the site is becoming easier to crawl, clearer to users and more complete against search intent.

The World Wide Web Consortium is relevant where site quality, accessibility and technical standards affect how pages are built. The Australian Competition and Consumer Commission is relevant for keeping marketing claims fair and accurate.

  1. Collect the baseline. Prepare the site URL, main services, target suburbs or markets, and any current traffic or ranking reports before requesting an audit.
  2. Read the audit by priority. Separate crawl, indexation and page experience blockers from content gaps and link opportunities.
  3. Map scope to budget. Compare the proposed monthly work against the stated retainer range and the number of keywords or pages being targeted.
  4. Set review points. Use monthly completed-work reporting and the 3 to 6 month or 9 to 12 month timing bands as checkpoints, not guarantees.
