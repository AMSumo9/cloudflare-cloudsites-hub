Concrete Driveways Toowoomba Explained

The published guidance from Concreting Toowoomba places ground conditions at the beginning of the decision. Reactive black soil wets and dries, while sloping blocks can change the amount of preparation needed. A quote that only describes the visible concrete surface may not explain the work below it.

Before requesting prices, record the approximate area, intended use, existing surface and the easiest route for access. Note whether the project is a new driveway, a replacement, a side path or a slab. Photos can show the approach, slope, edge conditions and any old concrete.

  • Describe the suburb, job type and rough size.
  • Point out slopes, narrow access, existing concrete and areas where water needs to move away from the house.
  • Ask how reactive soil affects preparation, reinforcement and curing for the proposed work.

Choose the finish after the function is clear

Function should lead the finish decision. A plain replacement driveway may prioritise access and a straightforward surface, while exposed aggregate, coloured or stamped concrete changes the appearance of a driveway, path or entertaining area. Resurfacing can be considered when the slab underneath remains sound, using an overlay or coating.

Paths and patios need more than a surface choice. The published service information describes falls that shed water away from the house, so ask how levels will be set at doors, side access and edges. For an older stained or dull surface, cleaning and sealing may be relevant for driveways, paths and exposed aggregate, but the existing condition still needs to be assessed.

Ask each quote to name the proposed finish and where it applies. That makes alternatives easier to compare when one option includes an overlay or coating and another proposes removal and replacement.

Compare the two common project paths

Driveways and slabs can look similar on a quote, but their site questions differ. A driveway is closely tied to the property frontage, crossover and road authority. A house, shed, garage or patio slab is more concerned with the intended structure, levels, access and the ground supporting the slab.

Use the comparison below to keep the first conversation focused. It does not replace a site assessment, and the required approval depends on the property and the proposed work.

  • For a frontage project, ask who controls the road and what crossover guidance applies.
  • For a slab, explain whether it is for a house, shed, garage or patio and what the area must support.
  • For either project, ask what preparation, reinforcement, thickness, finish and curing approach is included.

Approvals and licensing checks

Approval is a property question, not a box to leave until the pour is booked. Toowoomba Regional Council treats some driveways as accepted development, while others need a permit. The homeowner remains responsible for having required building approvals sorted before concreting begins.

A crossover onto a state-controlled road may sit outside the normal council driveway process. Confirm which authority controls the road, then check the relevant driveway guidance for the documents and details required. The application timeframe depends on the application and council's current processing, so line up the start date around approval rather than assuming it will be immediate.

For work worth more than $3,300, Queensland concreting work requires the appropriate QBCC concreting licence. The published guidance also says most residential work above that value needs Home Warranty Scheme cover arranged by the contractor. Ask to see the relevant licence and confirm what cover applies before signing.

Make the quote useful before you sign

A useful quote should let you compare the parts that are easy to overlook. Ask whether it includes removal of old concrete, site preparation, reinforcement, thickness, finish, access limitations and any approval-related work. If the site has reactive soil or a slope, ask which allowance addresses that condition.

Keep the description consistent when comparing options. Give each concreter the same rough size, photos and intended use, then ask for exclusions in writing. Different inclusions can make figures difficult to compare, so request a clear scope before choosing an option.

  1. Confirm the proposed area, thickness and finish.
  2. Check preparation, reinforcement and curing are described.
  3. Ask who handles approvals and what documents are needed.
  4. Verify the QBCC licence and any applicable residential cover.
  5. Keep the quote, scope and approval records together before work begins.
  1. Describe the site. Send the suburb, job type, rough size, intended use, access details and photos of the existing area.
  2. Check the road authority. For a driveway, confirm whether council guidance applies or whether a state-controlled road changes the process.
  3. Ask for a complete scope. Request clear notes on preparation, reinforcement, thickness, finish, removal and approvals.
  4. Verify credentials. For work above $3,300, check that the concreter holds the right QBCC licence and ask about applicable residential cover.
  5. Compare before booking. Review exclusions and align the start date with any required approval and the agreed site preparation.

Common questions

What changes the cost of a driveway or slab? The published guidance identifies area, thickness, reinforcement, finish, access and site preparation as cost factors. Removing old concrete and choosing exposed aggregate can also change the figure.

Does every Toowoomba driveway need council approval? No. Toowoomba Regional Council treats some driveways as accepted development, while others need a permit. Check the driveway guidance for the property before booking work.

What if the property fronts a state-controlled road? The crossover may fall outside normal council driveway rules. Confirm which authority controls the road and arrange any required approval before concreting starts.

Can an old driveway be resurfaced? Resurfacing may suit a slab that is still sound, using an overlay or coating. The existing slab should be assessed before choosing that option.

What should be included in a quote? Ask for the area, thickness, reinforcement, finish, access, site preparation, removal, approvals and exclusions. This makes different proposals easier to compare.
