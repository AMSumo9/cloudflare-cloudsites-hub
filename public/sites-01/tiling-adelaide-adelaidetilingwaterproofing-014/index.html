Tiling Adelaide Explained

Adelaide Tiling & Waterproofing frames tile work as a combined visible and concealed project: the grout line, tile layout and finish only perform properly when the base, falls, movement and wet-area detailing have been considered together. That is a useful lens for any owner preparing a brief, because a neat surface can hide unresolved substrate or waterproofing questions.

The source page points to several inputs a specialist needs before a scope can be compared: the existing surface, the level of water exposure, fixtures, drainage and the tile format. Those details help separate what can be estimated from supplied information from what must be inspected on site.

  • Describe whether the work is in a bathroom, ensuite, laundry, kitchen, living area, entry, feature wall, outdoor area or alfresco zone.
  • Note symptoms such as leaking, cracking, loose tiles or deteriorated shower finishes when repair is the reason for the enquiry.
  • Ask that waterproofing, screeding and preparation are described separately where they apply.

Who This Applies To

This guide applies to owners planning new wall and floor tiles, wet-area waterproofing, bathroom renovation interfaces, shower repairs, regrouting, kitchen and laundry splashbacks, interior floors, feature walls, and outdoor or alfresco tile finishes. It is also relevant when the project is still at briefing stage and the owner needs to decide what information to collect before comparing written scopes.

The same checklist does not suit every job. A leaking shower needs diagnosis before regrouting is treated as the answer. A planned bathroom renovation needs coordination of demolition, plumbing and electrical rough-ins, floor falls, membrane boundaries, cabinetry, fixtures and finished floor heights. An outdoor area needs exposure, drainage, slip needs and movement considered before the finish is selected.

Questions To Ask Before Comparing Quotes

A useful quote should make preparation visible. The source page says inclusions such as preparation, tile format, layout, trims, grout, movement joints, access and waste should be identified, with waterproofing or screeding separately described where required. That protects the comparison from becoming a simple finish-price exercise.

Ask each provider to explain what assumptions sit behind the price. In a wet area, that includes the substrate, falls, wastes, penetrations, thresholds and membrane system. In a tiled floor or wall outside a wet area, it includes substrate preparation, adjoining transitions and whether movement joints need to be allowed for in the set-out.

  • What must be inspected before a fixed scope is confirmed?
  • Which parts are preparation, which parts are waterproofing, and which parts are tiling?
  • What exclusions could change the scope after demolition or surface removal?
  • Who confirms licensed work details and product specifications in writing?

Repair Or Renovation

The source page draws a clear distinction between maintenance and renovation. Moisture symptoms may relate to sealant, plumbing, substrate movement or the membrane system. Regrouting may suit a maintenance issue, but it should not be treated as a universal leak repair without diagnosis.

A renovation has a wider sequence. Before demolition, record the room condition, suspected moisture, access limits, hazardous-material questions and fixtures that may move. Before waterproofing, confirm substrate condition, falls, wastes, penetrations, thresholds and the membrane system. Before tiling, settle tile modules, niches, trims, movement joints, cabinetry lines and adjoining floor transitions together.

That order matters because the final tile set-out is affected by work completed earlier. A changed waste position, cabinet line or threshold detail can alter cuts, falls and transitions, so those interfaces belong in the scope before products are ordered.

Local Conditions Worth Raising

Locality is useful when it changes a practical question. The source page gives examples rather than a suburb checklist: apartment access in the city, coastal exposure at Glenelg and Henley Beach, older villa substrates through Norwood and Unley, and newer slabs around Mawson Lakes or Mount Barker can affect preparation and sequencing.

Those examples should be used as prompts for the briefing conversation. An apartment job may need access and waste planning. A coastal or outdoor finish may need closer attention to exposure, drainage and slip needs. An older substrate may need more investigation before the finish is priced. A newer slab still needs the surface and movement assumptions checked before tile work proceeds.

Use the suburb or property type to explain the likely site condition, not as a substitute for inspection. The provider remains responsible for confirming the actual scope, licensing, products and trade coordination in writing.

  1. Describe the condition. Record the room, existing surface, visible symptoms, previous repairs and whether moisture is already visible.
  2. Separate the layers. Ask which assumptions relate to plumbing, substrate, waterproofing, screeding and the final tile finish.
  3. Compare written scopes. Check preparation, inclusions, exclusions, access, waste, licensed work details and product specifications before accepting a quote.
  4. Coordinate set-out. Confirm tile modules, trims, niches, movement joints, cabinetry lines and floor transitions before materials are ordered.

Common questions

Do tiles and grout make a shower waterproof? No. The source page states that tiles and grout are finishes, while the compliant membrane and correctly treated junctions form the waterproof layer behind them.

When is regrouting enough for a leaking shower? Regrouting may be appropriate for a maintenance issue, but the source page says moisture symptoms can also come from sealant, plumbing, substrate movement or the membrane system. Diagnosis should come before treating regrouting as the answer.

What should an Adelaide tile quote include? A useful quote should identify preparation, tile format, layout, trims, grout, movement joints, access and waste. Waterproofing or screeding should be separately described where required.

How long should waterproofing cure before tiles are installed? The source page does not give a universal waiting period. It says cure time depends on the membrane system, film thickness and site conditions, and that the installer should follow the product specification and record the system used.
