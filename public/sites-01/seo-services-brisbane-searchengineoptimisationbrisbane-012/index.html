Choosing the right work first

The service page names local SEO, technical SEO, content marketing and link building as separate work areas. Use those categories as a diagnosis tool. Local work fits when searches include suburb, map or nearby intent. Technical work fits when crawl, indexation, speed or structured data issues stop useful pages being understood. Content work fits when service pages do not answer the questions attached to commercial intent.

Authority work is described through digital PR, supplier mentions and resource placements. That should come after the site has pages worth citing. A related Brisbane search visibility guide provides another crawl path for the same topic.

  • Local fit: ask whether Google Business Profile, citations, reviews management or suburb-relevant content are needed.
  • Technical fit: ask which crawl, indexation, Core Web Vitals and mobile checks come first.
  • Content fit: ask how keyword-mapped pages will support enquiries.
  • Authority fit: ask which link earning methods are relevant to the business.

Reading reports without chasing noise

The published reporting measures are keyword movement, organic traffic and conversions. Each answers a different question. Keyword movement shows whether selected terms are gaining visibility. Organic traffic shows whether more searchers are reaching the site. Conversions show whether that visibility is producing enquiries or useful actions.

Read the three measures together. A page can attract more visitors without better enquiries if the intent is wrong. Enquiries can improve even when some rankings move around, especially where maps, local pack results and organic listings overlap. The review should stay tied to the agreed commercial goal.

The page also states that ranking guarantees are not offered. A responsible proposal can explain the work, assumptions and reporting method, but it should not promise positions that no provider controls.

Questions before approving work

A strong proposal should explain what will happen first and what evidence will shape the next step. The published page asks businesses to share their website, target suburbs and commercial goals so fit, scope and timing can be reviewed before work is recommended.

  1. What is the current baseline for organic traffic, keyword visibility and conversions?
  2. Which technical issues will be checked before content or authority work begins?
  3. Which local assets need attention, including Google Business Profile, citations or suburb pages?
  4. How will content topics be selected and mapped to commercial intent?
  5. What link building methods are proposed, and which placements are unsuitable?
  6. How will costs, timelines and deliverables be confirmed in writing?

If the first review finds technical blockers, fixing those may come before new content. If the site is technically sound but lacks useful service pages, keyword-mapped content may deserve priority.

  1. Share the baseline. Provide the website, target suburbs and growth goal so fit can be assessed before work is recommended.
  2. Confirm the first scope. Ask for the audit, technical checks, local visibility review or content plan to be defined in writing.
  3. Agree on measures. Set reporting around organic traffic, keyword movement and conversions rather than ranking promises.
  4. Review the next step. Use the first findings to decide whether ongoing local, technical, content or authority work is justified.

Common questions

How long can SEO take to show useful movement? The published page says technical fixes can show early movement within weeks, while meaningful organic growth usually needs several months of consistent work. Timing depends on current authority, content depth, competitor activity and implementation speed.

Is local SEO different from general SEO? Yes. The published explanation says local SEO focuses on suburb, map and nearby intent, including Google Business Profile work, service area content, local citations, reviews management and pages that explain real coverage.

Should a Brisbane business use SEO if it already runs Google Ads? The source material says many businesses use both. Paid search can capture demand quickly, while organic work builds a longer-term channel and can reduce reliance on paid clicks over time.
