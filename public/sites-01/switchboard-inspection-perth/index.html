Switchboard Inspection Perth: A Practical Homeowner Guide

Last updated: November 2026

For homeowners comparing switchboard inspection perth options, the useful starting point is a clear assessment of the existing board, the circuits it serves and the changes planned for the property.

Switchboard Inspection Perth Explained

The information published by Switchboard Upgrades Perth points to a decision based on the condition and capacity of the existing board, rather than on age alone. Repeated tripping, unexplained power loss at night and old ceramic fuses are reasons to have the installation assessed. An electrician tests the circuits and explains whether the work is limited to repairs or requires a new board.

The inspection should also identify whether the board has circuit breakers, safety switches and clear labels. RCDs, also called safety switches, cut power quickly when current leaks to earth, which lowers the risk of a serious shock. A crowded board or one with no spare room may need more capacity if the household is planning solar, air conditioning, an EV charger or extra circuits.

  • Ask which circuit is causing the repeated trip and whether the underlying wiring needs attention.
  • Confirm how many circuits will be moved, protected or relabelled.
  • Ask whether future equipment has been considered in the board layout.

Repair, partial work or a new board

There are several possible outcomes, and the quote should make the distinction visible. One faulty breaker can point to a repair. Ceramic fuses, no RCDs or no spare room can point towards a new board, subject to the electrician's assessment on site. A few safety switches may therefore be a different job from replacing the complete switchboard.

For a useful comparison, ask the electrician to state what remains in place, what is being replaced and what testing is included. The new installation described by the published guide uses circuit breakers and RCDs, with every circuit clearly labelled. Clear labelling is practical when a circuit trips or when another electrician later needs to trace the board.

Quote variables that change the job

The published Perth cost range is $800 to $2,500 for a standard home as at October 2026, but the amount for a particular property depends on the board size, number of circuits and wiring behind it. A written quote is more useful than a headline figure because it shows which conditions have been allowed for.

Ask whether the quote includes the board, circuit breakers, RCDs, circuit labels, testing and removal of the old equipment. If the backing panel may contain asbestos, ask how it will be assessed and whether panel replacement is included. Older boards can use a cement sheet backing panel, so this question should be settled before work begins rather than left as an assumption.

Meter box work can create another variable. If the work reaches the meter side, the electrician can explain whether Western Power needs to cut the supply first. Ask how the supply interruption will be planned and whether any additional meter-side work is included in the written scope.

Selling, leasing and planned additions

A sale or new lease changes the inspection question. The published guide states that a WA home sold or newly leased needs at least two RCDs protecting its power and lighting circuits. An older board may have none, so owners and agents should ask for the protection status to be recorded in the quote and completion paperwork.

Planned additions create a separate capacity question. Solar, air conditioning, an EV charger or extra circuits can require room in the board. Ask whether the proposed layout leaves suitable space and whether the existing circuits can support the intended use. This is especially relevant when a renovation or equipment installation is already being planned, because the board may otherwise need to be altered again.

Paperwork, timing and the handover

The work starts with an inspection and circuit testing, followed by a quote. During installation, the old board is replaced or altered, the circuits are connected to circuit breakers and RCDs where required, and the circuits are labelled. Power is off while the old board comes out and the new one is wired in, then it comes back on. The duration depends on the number of circuits and whether wiring needs repair, so the electrician should give the property-specific timeframe.

In Western Australia, electrical work must be completed by a person holding the right electrical licence. After installation work, the homeowner is owed an electrical safety certificate within 28 days. Keep the certificate with the quote and any documents describing asbestos-panel or meter-box work. These records can help explain what was completed when the property is later sold or leased.

  • Keep the written scope and final invoice together.
  • Check that circuit labels match the circuits tested.
  • Request the electrical safety certificate within the stated period.
  1. Describe the symptoms. Record repeated tripping, unexplained night-time power loss, ceramic fuses and any planned additions such as solar or an EV charger.
  2. Arrange circuit testing. Ask an electrician to inspect the board, test the circuits and identify whether the issue is a repair, partial upgrade or replacement.
  3. Compare the written scope. Check the circuit count, board equipment, RCD protection, labels, asbestos-panel assessment and any meter-side work.
  4. Collect the handover documents. Confirm the circuits are labelled and request the electrical safety certificate within 28 days of installation work.

Common questions

How much does a switchboard inspection or upgrade cost in Perth? The published guide gives a standard Perth home range of $800 to $2,500 as at October 2026. Board size, circuit count and the wiring behind the board affect the figure, so a written quote is needed for the property.

What signs mean an existing board should be assessed? Repeated tripping, unexplained power loss at night and old ceramic fuses are listed reasons for an assessment. Missing RCDs or no spare room can also affect whether replacement is recommended.

Will the power be off all day? Not necessarily. Power is off while the old board is removed and the new one is wired in, then it comes back on. The electrician can give a timeframe after seeing the board.

What should a quote mention if the board is old? Ask about circuit count, RCDs, circuit breakers, labels, wiring behind the board, possible asbestos in a cement sheet backing panel and whether meter-side work could involve Western Power.

What paperwork should a homeowner receive? After installation work in Western Australia, the homeowner is owed an electrical safety certificate within 28 days. Keep it with the quote and records describing the completed work.
