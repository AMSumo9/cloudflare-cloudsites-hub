Asset Finance Australia Comparison Explained

When evaluating asset finance australia options, the primary differentiator is who owns the asset during the loan term. The chattel mortgage is the most common structure for businesses seeking immediate ownership. You borrow the funds to purchase the equipment and take ownership from day one, while the lender registers a security interest over the item. This structure suits businesses that want to claim the GST upfront and claim depreciation on their tax return.

In contrast, a finance lease involves the financier purchasing the asset and leasing it to you for a fixed period. You pay for the use of the equipment, but the financier retains ownership. This structure keeps the asset off your balance sheet and offers predictable repayments, which can assist with business gearing ratios. A third option is the operating lease, or rental, which functions like a pure rental agreement with no obligation to buy the asset at the end. This is often preferred for fast-depreciating technology or equipment that requires regular refreshing.

Analysing the Costs

Comparing quotes requires looking beyond the advertised interest rate. The true cost of asset finance is shaped by several components including the deposit, the loan term and the balloon or residual payment. Many facilities finance 100 percent of the purchase price, though contributing a deposit can often secure a lower interest rate. The term generally ranges from one to seven years, usually matched to the expected productive life of the asset.

A balloon payment is a lump sum owed at the end of the term. While this significantly reduces your monthly repayments during the loan, it requires a large final payment or refinancing. Lenders also assess the specific asset type and age. Newer, mainstream equipment typically attracts sharper rates than specialised or older machinery. Your business profile, including trading history and credit strength, also plays a critical role in the final rate offered.

Tax and Balance Sheet Treatment

The choice of structure dictates how the loan affects your tax position and financial statements. With a chattel mortgage, the asset sits on your balance sheet. You can typically claim the GST on the purchase price as a lump sum in your next Business Activity Statement (BAS) and claim interest charges and depreciation as tax deductions. This is often beneficial for businesses that want to maximise deductions and own the equipment outright.

A finance lease treats the payments as operating expenses. Because the financier owns the asset, it does not appear as an asset or liability on your balance sheet, which can improve financial ratios like debt-to-equity. You cannot claim depreciation, but the lease payments are generally tax-deductible. Businesses must weigh the cash flow benefits of keeping the asset off-balance sheet against the long-term benefit of ownership and capital allowances.

Suitability by Industry and Asset

Asset finance is versatile but best applied when the equipment directly generates revenue. Trades and construction firms frequently use these facilities for utes, trucks, excavators and earthmoving plant. Manufacturers utilise the loans for CNC machinery and industrial equipment to expand production capacity without tying up cash reserves.

Transport and logistics operators rely on it to fund fleet upgrades, while medical, dental and veterinary practices use it to fit out clinics with expensive diagnostic and treatment gear. Hospitality and retail businesses often fund fit-outs and point-of-sale systems through these channels. The common factor across these industries is that the equipment serves as a revenue-generating tool, ensuring the repayments are aligned with the income the asset produces.

Key takeaway

Asset finance allows businesses to fund equipment over 1 to 7 years using the item as security. The three main structures are chattel mortgages, finance leases and rentals, each differing in ownership, tax treatment and balance sheet impact. While chattel mortgages offer immediate ownership and GST benefits, finance leases provide predictable payments and keep assets off the balance sheet. Costs depend on risk, asset age and the residual value.

Common questions

What is the main difference between a chattel mortgage and a finance lease? The main difference is ownership. With a chattel mortgage, you own the asset from the start and can claim GST and depreciation. With a finance lease, the financier owns the asset, and you lease it, keeping it off your balance sheet.

Can I finance 100 percent of the asset value? Yes, many asset finance facilities fund the full purchase price. However, paying a deposit can sometimes reduce the interest rate offered by the lender.

What assets are eligible for this type of finance? Any tangible, identifiable item with resale value can usually be financed. This includes vehicles, machinery, medical equipment, office fit-outs and agricultural plant.
