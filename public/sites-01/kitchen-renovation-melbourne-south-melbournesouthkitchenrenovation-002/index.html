Choose the scope before choosing finishes

A full project can remove old cabinets, coordinate the trades behind the walls and install new benchtops, while a smaller job may focus on compact storage and additional preparation space. Flat pack cabinets can suit a budget-conscious brief, whereas custom joinery can be shaped around the room, including tall pantry walls or a butler's pantry.

Natural stone affects more than the surface selection. The sink, cooktop and splashback need to be considered in the layout before the benchtop is cut. A 3D design can help a household review circulation, cabinet proportions and working space before construction begins. Ask which elements are fixed, which are allowances, and which decisions could change the quote.

Make the written quote useful

The strongest quote is not just a total. It identifies cabinets, benchtops, plumbing, electrical work, tiling, painting and other agreed items, then separates allowances for issues that may only become visible after demolition. Older homes can reveal walls and pipework once the cabinets are removed, so the scope should explain how an unknown will be discussed and approved.

Cost comparisons are difficult when one proposal includes trade work and another only prices cabinetry. Ask whether the layout stays in place, whether plumbing or electrical positions move, and whether custom cabinets add lead time. A clear price before work starts gives the household a defined basis for comparing options, without pretending that every hidden condition can be known in advance.

Sequence the trades around the installation

Trade order affects both timing and rework. Plumbing and electrical rough-in are completed before walls close and cabinets are installed. Cabinetry then needs to fit the measured room, while the tiler generally works later in the sequence. Painting and final fitting complete the visible result.

  1. Confirm the layout, measurements, appliances and written scope.
  2. Remove the old kitchen and inspect walls, pipework and access conditions.
  3. Complete required plumbing and electrical work before the walls close.
  4. Install cabinetry, benchtops and coordinated fixtures.
  5. Finish tiling, painting and checks against the agreed scope.

A kitchen that keeps its existing layout may finish sooner than one with moved plumbing or new walls. Custom cabinets can add lead time before installation. Ask for a schedule that states how many days the household may be without a working kitchen, while recognising that the actual sequence depends on the selected scope.

Who this guide applies to

This guide suits owners of older kitchens, households short of storage or bench space, and people comparing a compact update with a more extensive rebuild. It is also relevant when the project may include a laundry or vanity, or when the choice is between flat pack fitting and custom cabinetry.

Before accepting a proposal, ask who is responsible for coordinating the cabinet maker, plumber, electrician and tiler. In Victoria, the published business guidance says regulated plumbing and electrical work must be completed by licensed trades who issue certificates. It also says a registered building practitioner or building company should run a combined job, with the quote naming who performs each part.

Ask separately about product warranties for cabinets, benchtops and appliances, the contractor's workmanship warranty, and Australian Consumer Law guarantees for fixed items such as stoves, sinks, tapware and extractor fans.

  1. Describe the current problem. Record failing doors, missing storage, limited bench space and any access issue affecting the room.
  2. Confirm the layout brief. Identify appliances, sink, cooktop, pantry needs and whether plumbing or electrical positions may move.
  3. Compare written scopes. Check that cabinets, benchtops, trades, finishes and allowances for hidden issues are listed.
  4. Check responsibility. Ask who coordinates each trade and who provides certificates for regulated work.
  5. Request the schedule. Confirm the expected trade order and how long the household may lack a working kitchen.

Common questions

What affects the cost of a kitchen project? Room size, materials and whether plumbing or electrical positions move are key cost factors. Flat pack cabinets generally sit at the lower end of the described options, while custom joinery with natural stone sits higher.

How long can the work take? Timing depends on the scope. Keeping the existing layout may finish sooner than moving plumbing or adding walls, and custom cabinetry can add lead time. Ask for a written schedule.

What should a quote include? It should identify the fittings and trades, state the agreed scope, and include an allowance or process for hidden issues in an older home.

Who should coordinate the job? For work combining cabinetry, plumbing, electrical and tiling, the published guidance recommends a registered building practitioner or building company, with responsibility named in the quote.

What warranty questions should be asked? Ask about manufacturer warranties for products, the contractor's workmanship warranty, and Australian Consumer Law guarantees applying to fixed items such as sinks, stoves, tapware and extractor fans.
