Private Lenders Australia Explained

Bottom Line Finance frames private lending as commercial, business purpose capital from non-bank and private lenders, secured against property or other assets. That makes the first filter purpose, not price. A borrower looking for an owner occupied home loan is outside the published scope. A developer, asset backed business borrower or commercial property borrower may have a live reason to compare non-bank structures.

The source material points to deals that do not tick standard bank boxes. That may include timing gaps, construction programs, completed stock that is still unsold, subordinated capital behind a senior lender, or property development funding that needs attention to land, build and cost to completion. The assessment should begin with the project, the numbers and the timeline, because those are the inputs the provider asks borrowers to send.

The Deal Types To Separate

Private capital is not one product. The published service list separates short term bridging finance from development finance, mezzanine finance, residual stock loans and second mortgage style structures. A borrower should avoid treating these as interchangeable, because each one answers a different funding problem.

  • Bridging finance: ask what timing gap needs to close, such as settling a site before a sale completes.
  • Construction loans: ask how drawdowns will match the build program as work is completed.
  • Residual stock loans: ask whether completed dwellings are unsold after practical completion and whether equity is tied up.
  • Mezzanine finance: ask where the extra debt sits in the capital stack, behind the senior lender and ahead of equity.
  • Development finance: ask whether land, build and cost to completion need to be considered together.

Those questions are more useful than asking for the cheapest quote first. The source page states that the cheapest rate is rarely the best outcome and that value is weighed against return on equity, time, risk and future deal capacity.

This guide applies to commercial and business purpose borrowers with a property backed or asset backed funding question. It is most relevant where the published categories match the underlying asset, residential development, commercial property, industrial property, specialised assets, childcare or farm and agribusiness.

What To Prepare Before A Conversation

The provider asks for the project, location, amount and timeline. A stronger enquiry should also explain the purpose of funds, the security asset, existing debt, exit plan and the issue that makes bank funding difficult. That does not require glossy packaging, but it does require enough detail for someone to test whether the structure can work.

For private lenders australia discussions, useful preparation includes current valuations if available, development budgets where relevant, build program details for construction, sales evidence for residual stock, lease or operator detail for income producing specialised assets, and a plain English statement of what must happen next. Where information is missing, mark it as missing rather than guessing. A clean gap is easier to assess than a confident number that later changes.

How To Compare Structure, Not Just Rate

The page's core editorial stance is value over price. In practice, that means the borrower should compare the role of capital in the deal. A higher cost facility may still be unsuitable if it blocks the exit, clashes with the senior lender or weakens the next transaction. A lower cost option may also be poor value if it arrives too late for settlement or does not match the drawdown pattern.

Use the conversation to test fit: what asset supports the loan, where the lender sits in the capital stack, how funds are released, what evidence is needed before approval, what happens if sales or completion timing shifts, and whether the proposed structure protects equity or simply adds cost. The published page says no deal is better than a bad deal, so a refusal can be useful information rather than a failed enquiry.

  1. Define the purpose. State whether the funds are for a commercial, business purpose, development, bridge, construction or other asset backed need.
  2. Map the security. List the property or asset supporting the request, existing debt and any senior lender position.
  3. Set the timeline. Describe the settlement, build, sale or completion event that drives the required funding date.
  4. Match the facility type. Compare the need against private lending, construction, bridging, mezzanine, residual stock or development finance.
  5. Ask about value. Test cost against equity protection, return on equity, risk and the ability to complete the next deal.
