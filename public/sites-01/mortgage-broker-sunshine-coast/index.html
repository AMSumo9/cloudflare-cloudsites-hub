Mortgage Broker Sunshine Coast Explained

Home Loan Broker Sunshine Coast is presented as a review path for Sunshine Coast borrowers who want lender options compared before they apply. The source page says the review is free, there is no direct fee for a standard home-loan review, and any commission, fee or clawback arrangement must be disclosed in writing before a borrower commits.

The service description focuses on fit, not only rate shopping. It says income, deposit and goals are mapped against a broad lender panel that can include big four banks, second-tier lenders, mutuals and non-banks. It also says the broker handles credit advice and the application end to end.

A borrower should use that scope to leave the review with a lender shortlist, missing document list and a realistic timeline. Rate, fees, loan splits, deposit position and settlement readiness all belong in the same conversation.

Who this applies to

This guide is for borrowers who already have a purchase, refinance or loan-structure question and need to know what to ask before choosing a path. The source page names first home buyers, refinancers, investment borrowers and construction borrowers, so the review should be framed around the borrower type rather than a generic loan enquiry.

  • First home buyers: ask how deposit gaps, genuine savings, the Queensland First Home Owner Grant and the Australian Government 5% Deposit Scheme are treated for the intended purchase.
  • Refinancers: ask whether the current rate, fees and structure make a switch worthwhile before signing new loan documents.
  • Investors: ask which lenders can consider interest-only terms, offset accounts, SMSF lending or multiple-property structures.
  • Construction borrowers: ask what the lender needs before approval and what must be ready before settlement or progress payments.

Documents and borrowing power

The published checklist asks for two recent payslips, three months of personal account statements, identification, current loan or rent details, and an idea of the property type. Borrowers should also be ready to explain dependants, ongoing commitments and any planned change to income or property use, because those inputs affect serviceability.

The page says borrowing power depends on net income, ongoing commitments, dependants and lender policy. It also says most Sunshine Coast households it works with qualify for $450,000 to $900,000 on standard owner-occupier terms. Treat that range as an indication only. The same page says a precise number needs live serviceability across lender policy.

The borrowing power calculator is described as a no email gate starting point. Use it to check whether a planned price range is worth discussing, then ask the broker to test the real lender shortlist, deposit position and timing.

First home buyer deposit checks

The first home buyer material names the Queensland First Home Owner Grant, the Australian Government 5% Deposit Scheme, deposit gaps and lender selection. It also says eligible buyers may use the 5% Deposit Scheme with no lenders mortgage insurance, with the government guaranteeing part of the loan.

The same page says income caps and place caps were removed from 1 October 2025, but property price caps still apply and should be confirmed before contracting. A buyer should not treat scheme access as settled until the property, price and lender treatment have been checked.

Useful questions include:

  • Will grant funds be treated as part of the deposit for this purchase?
  • Is lenders mortgage insurance payable, reduced or removed under the proposed structure?
  • What savings evidence is needed before application?
  • Does the property price sit inside the relevant scheme cap?

Refinance, investor and structure checks

A refinance review should compare the existing rate, fees and structure against available lender options. The source page says the broker flags whether a switch is worth it before anything is signed. Ask for the keep-or-switch reasoning in writing or in a clear summary, especially where fees, loan splits or future flexibility are part of the decision.

For investors, the page names interest-only loans, offset accounts, SMSF lending and multiple-property structures. Treat those as suitability questions, not automatic recommendations. Ask which lenders support the structure, what documents are required, and whether the proposed loan still fits the borrower's wider property plan.

ASIC Moneysmart's home loan guidance encourages borrowers to compare features, fees and repayments, not just the interest rate. Its property investment material also points borrowers toward costs and risk, so an investment review should include repayment capacity as well as lender selection.

What a useful review should produce

The source page sets out a four-step process: submit a short form, receive a broker callback, complete a review that confirms borrowing power, lender shortlist and realistic timeline, then proceed through application and settlement support. It also says annual rate reviews remain available after settlement.

The review should produce actions that can be checked. That may mean missing documents, a deposit issue, a shortlist of lenders to test, questions for a conveyancer, or a reason to delay the application. For Maroochydore, Caloundra or Buderim borrowers, the local detail that matters is the contract, property type, price and document readiness, not the suburb name on its own.

Before committing, ask for the written disclosure on commission, fees and clawback arrangements. The source page says brokers are generally paid by the lender when a loan settles, and disclosure should happen before the borrower commits.

  1. Define the loan goal. State whether the review is for a first purchase, refinance, investment loan or construction finance.
  2. Prepare documents. Have payslips, three months of account statements, ID, rent or current loan details, and property type notes ready.
  3. Test borrowing power. Use the calculator as an indication, then ask for live lender serviceability in the review.
  4. Compare structure. Ask how rate, fees, loan splits, offset use, LMI and settlement timing affect the shortlist.
  5. Confirm disclosures. Before committing, ask for written disclosure of commission, fees and any clawback arrangements.
