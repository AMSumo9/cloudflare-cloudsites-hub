Who this applies to

This guide applies to borrowers who want a structured conversation before choosing a lender. It is especially relevant where the application is not just a simple rate comparison, such as buying a first home, refinancing after circumstances change, borrowing for an investment property, building in a growth corridor, or applying with business income.

  • First home buyers: ask how deposit size, LMI, guarantor support and available schemes may change the options.
  • Refinancers: compare the current loan against the market, but also check fees, equity release and whether fixed or variable features still fit.
  • Investors: discuss loan structure, offsets, serviceability and the difference between principal and interest or interest-only repayment choices.
  • Self-employed borrowers: prepare income documents early and ask whether full-doc or alt-doc options are more realistic.
  • Construction borrowers: ask how progress payments, valuation steps and lender requirements are handled before committing to a build contract.

How to compare broker conversations

A useful broker conversation should make the trade-offs visible. The source page says brokers are usually paid by the lender for most standard home loans, while complex scenarios may involve a fee that should be disclosed in writing up front. That means the borrower should ask how the broker is paid, which lenders are being compared, and why one option is being recommended over another.

For a Western Sydney home loan search, avoid treating the lowest advertised rate as the whole answer. Ask about offset accounts, fixed versus variable rates, lenders mortgage insurance, repayment flexibility, application evidence and what happens from application through to settlement. ASIC Moneysmart also encourages borrowers to understand home loan features, costs and risks before deciding. The stronger outcome is not just a sharper rate, it is a loan structure that matches the next few years of ownership.

Application evidence and risk checks

Before submitting an application, a borrower should gather income evidence, current debt details, living expenses, savings history and the planned property use. The source page says borrowing power depends on income, existing debts, living expenses and lender serviceability assessment. It also notes that lenders commonly assess expenses against declared spending or the Household Expenditure Measure, then test repayments with the APRA buffer.

For low-deposit scenarios, check LMI early. The source page describes LMI as a one-off premium that protects the lender if the borrower defaults, and says it is usually relevant when borrowing more than 80% of the property value. Borrowers should ask whether LMI is paid upfront or capitalised, whether a 20% deposit, guarantor or eligible government pathway could avoid it, and whether the higher loan amount still fits the household budget.

  1. Define the loan goal. State whether the priority is buying, refinancing, investing, building, bridging or borrowing with self-employed income.
  2. Prepare the evidence. Collect income, debts, expenses, savings history and current loan documents before comparing lender options.
  3. Ask about remuneration. Request written disclosure of broker payment and any fee that may apply for complex lending.
  4. Compare structure, not just rate. Review offset, fixed or variable terms, LMI, equity release and settlement requirements before choosing.
  5. Check the next step. Confirm what the broker will do from application through to settlement and what you need to supply.

Common questions

Do brokers usually charge borrowers directly? The source page says most standard home loans do not involve a direct broker fee because the broker is typically paid by the lender after settlement. It also says complex scenarios may involve a fee, which should be disclosed in writing up front.

When does LMI usually become part of the conversation? The source page says lenders usually require LMI when borrowing more than 80% of the property value. It describes LMI as lender protection, not personal protection for the borrower.

What should a first home buyer ask first? Start with deposit evidence, LMI, whether a guarantor is involved, and whether any NSW or federal first home buyer support may apply. The exact eligibility should be checked against current scheme rules and lender approval.

Is refinancing only about getting a lower rate? No. The source page frames refinancing as reviewing the current loan against the market to cut rate, restructure or release equity, so fees, loan features and the reason for changing loans also matter.
