Contracts, permits and compliant trades

The provider says projects run under a written contract, with the right permits and registered plumbing and electrical trades in line with Victorian building requirements. It also states that domestic building work over $10,000 requires a written major domestic building contract under the Domestic Building Contracts Act 1995.

A useful contract does more than record the total price. It should set out inclusions, exclusions, payment stages and the variation process. If cabinetry, stone, appliances or splashback are included, ask what has been allowed for and whether later selections change the price.

Domestic building insurance should be checked before signing. The published page says it applies in Victoria where the contract value is above the current threshold, and notes that public guidance in June 2026 commonly cited more than $16,000, with reforms due from 1 July 2026. Materials and layout decisions

The provider says layout, materials and lighting are planned before cabinetry is made, with drawings shown before construction begins. That order helps keep changes on paper rather than on site, where cabinet and benchtop decisions are harder to unwind.

Benchtop selection now needs early discussion. The published page says engineered stone with 1% or more crystalline silica has been banned for manufacture, supply, processing and installation across Australia since 1 July 2024. It lists porcelain, sintered stone, natural stone, laminate, stainless steel, solid timber and compliant low-silica alternatives as options discussed with homeowners.

Cabinetry choices should follow daily use. Ask where appliances will sit, how pantry storage works, whether drawers or cupboards suit the household, and how lighting supports food preparation. In older homes, ask how joinery will meet retained character details, existing openings or uneven walls.

Quote questions before you proceed

A fixed price is reliable only when the fixed scope is visible. The provider says each project starts with a measure-up and an itemised written quote, so compare quotes by the work included, not just the final number.

  1. What is included in demolition, removal and site clean-up?
  2. Are cabinetry, benchtops, splashback, plumbing, electrical work and appliance installation included?
  3. Which materials are specified, and which are still subject to selection?
  4. What drawings will be approved before manufacturing starts?
  5. How are changes priced and approved after the contract is signed?
  6. What warranty and compliance paperwork is handed over at completion?

The published process is measure-up, design and quote, build and install, then handover. Use the stated 6 to 12 week full-renovation range as a planning guide, then ask how your own scope affects the schedule.

  1. Define the scope. Decide whether the kitchen needs a refresh, full rebuild, custom joinery or period-sensitive work before comparing quotes.
  2. Book a measure-up. Use the in-home measure-up to confirm dimensions, services, layout limits and household use.
  3. Approve drawings. Review layout, materials and lighting before cabinetry or benchtops are made.
  4. Check the contract. Confirm scope, price, payment stages, variations, permits and trade responsibilities before signing.
  5. Plan the handover. Ask what warranty and compliance paperwork will be provided at completion.
