What a useful fault description includes

A short, accurate description is more useful than a guessed cause. Note whether the problem affects one fitting, a room or a wider part of the home, and whether it started after an appliance or lighting change. A repeated trip should be assessed rather than treated as a reason to keep resetting the switch.

For a power point, fan or light, identify the proposed position and what the fitting will serve. Product supply, ceiling access, controls and finishing can change the work involved. For a switchboard question, ask whether the recommendation relates to a new load, a protection concern or an optional upgrade, and request a written scope that distinguishes those items.

  • Describe the result you need, such as restoring a failed fitting or adding power for equipment.
  • State whether the property is a unit or house and mention building access or a property manager where relevant.
  • Ask what testing is included at completion and how extra work must be approved.

Compare the quote behind the total

A clear quote should let you connect the recommendation to the problem or improvement you described. Ask whether the total includes the fitting or other supplied parts, access work, controls, finishing, testing and removal of replaced items. If a larger board or circuit proposal appears, ask which part is necessary for the stated job and which part is an optional improvement.

Extra work should have an approval path. Before accepting, ask how changes are communicated, who authorises them and whether the price is confirmed before the additional work starts. The published guidance says nothing goes ahead until the visit and price are agreed, which gives the homeowner a straightforward checkpoint.

Two quotes are easier to compare when they use the same description of the job. Put the requested fitting position, intended use, access constraints and testing expectations in each enquiry, then compare inclusions rather than totals alone.

Prepare the enquiry and close-out questions

For non-urgent work, the enquiry can begin with the fault or fitting, suburb and desired result. Add the best contact time, property-manager details and building-access requirements if they affect attendance. Availability, the visit and the price are separate decisions, so a callback request is not approval to proceed.

Before work is agreed, ask these practical questions:

  1. What does the proposed work include, and what is excluded?
  2. Will the contractor supply the product, or should the homeowner provide it?
  3. What access, controls or finishing assumptions affect the quote?
  4. How will extra work be approved?
  5. What testing or confirmation is provided when the job is complete?

For an urgent fault, follow the emergency guidance rather than waiting for a form response. For a planned job, a precise description and written scope give the homeowner a better basis for comparing options.

  1. Classify the situation. Separate immediate danger from a recurring fault or a planned fitting before choosing an enquiry route.
  2. Record the useful facts. Note the affected area, timing, property type, suburb, access conditions and intended result.
  3. Describe the requested work. For an installation, identify the position, product responsibility, controls and what the fitting will serve.
  4. Review the scope. Ask what is included, how extra work is approved and what testing comes at completion.
  5. Agree before starting. Confirm the visit and price separately, then proceed only once the proposed work is accepted.

Common questions

What should I do if there is immediate electrical danger? Keep clear if there is electric shock, fire or a fallen powerline, and call Triple Zero on 000. Do not wait for a routine callback.

What information helps with a tripping switch? Record when it trips, which area or fitting is affected and whether anything changed beforehand. Leave diagnosis and wiring investigation to a licensed electrical contractor.

Can one power point or fan be discussed as a small job? Yes. State where it is wanted and what it will serve. Product supply, access, controls and finishing may affect the scope.

How should I compare electrical quotes? Compare the written inclusions, product responsibility, access assumptions, testing and extra-work approval process, not only the final total.
