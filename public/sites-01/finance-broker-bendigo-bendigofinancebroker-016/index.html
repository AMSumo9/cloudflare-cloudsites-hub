Choosing the right loan pathway

The published options include chattel mortgage, finance lease and hire purchase for equipment finance. It also notes balloon payments, flexible terms and seasonal payment options for farmers. Those choices can change ownership, repayments and end-of-term handling.

A chattel mortgage is described as a structure where the borrower owns the asset from day one, may claim GST upfront, and can benefit from depreciation deductions. A finance lease is described differently: the lender owns the asset during the lease, GST is charged on repayments, and the borrower may return or buy the asset at the end.

Tax treatment should be checked with an accountant before signing. A broker can compare lender options, but the structure still needs to match the accounts, cash flow and expected asset life. A transport operator buying a used trailer may ask different questions from a professional services firm financing an office fitout.

Documents and timing questions

The published process has three stages: explain the goal, compare options, then choose and settle. Pre-approval is described as often available within 24 hours for standard applications, while full approval is described as typically taking 2 to 5 business days depending on complexity and document completeness.

A complete file is more than a contact form. For equipment and business finance, prepare recent financial information, the asset quote, business identification details, the intended use of funds and any existing finance commitments. For a smaller unsecured loan, ask whether same-day approval is possible, because the business says some smaller unsecured loans offer it.

Before lodgement, ask whether the asset itself will be enough security, whether a balloon payment is proposed, what happens if settlement is delayed, and whether the lender needs extra information because revenue is seasonal or the business is replacing an older asset.

Broker fees, security and comparison checks

The published fee position is $0 broker fees. For most equipment finance, the business says the equipment itself serves as security, with additional security rarely required for standard applications and unsecured options also available.

Treat those points as application-dependent. A lender still needs to assess the borrower, asset, documents and loan purpose. Before accepting an offer, compare the repayment amount, term, balloon payment if any, security requested, fees, early payout position and conditions needed for settlement.

Ask the broker to explain why a recommended lender fits the asset and the business purpose. A lower monthly repayment may reflect a balloon, a longer term or a different structure. That may be suitable, but it should be clear before the documents are signed.

  1. Define the purpose. Write down the asset, supplier, price or working-capital need before asking for quotes.
  2. Prepare the evidence. Gather financials, identification, asset details and existing finance commitments so the application is complete.
  3. Compare structures. Ask how chattel mortgage, finance lease, hire purchase, balloon payments or unsecured options would change repayments and ownership.
  4. Check advice boundaries. Use the broker for lender comparison and speak with an accountant about tax treatment before signing.
  5. Confirm settlement steps. Before accepting an offer, confirm approval conditions, documents, security and timing through to settlement.
