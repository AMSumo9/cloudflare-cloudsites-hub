Choose the scope before comparing prices

Price comparisons are meaningful only when the same work is being compared. A basic flat pack project may sit at a different end of the range from custom joinery with natural stone, particularly when plumbing or electrical positions move. A useful quote should identify cabinets, benchtops, appliances where relevant, plumbing, electrical work, tiling, painting and removal of the old kitchen.

Older houses need room for unknown conditions. Once cabinets are removed, walls and pipework become easier to inspect, but a written scope cannot remove every possible issue. Ask how hidden work will be handled, what allowance is included and which changes require approval before extra work begins. Plan the sequence around the trades

The order of work affects both the schedule and the time without a working kitchen. The published process places plumbing and electrical rough-in before walls close up, cabinets before the finishing stages, and the tiler last. That sequence gives each trade a defined handover and reduces the chance of cabinets being installed before services are ready.

A project that keeps its layout can finish sooner than one involving moved plumbing or new walls, while custom cabinets add lead time before installation. Ask for a written schedule that identifies demolition, rough-in, cabinet delivery, installation, benchtop work and finishing. If the home will be without a functioning kitchen, confirm which days are expected to be disruptive and which decisions could change the order.

For a combined project, the quote should name the person or company responsible for coordinating cabinetry, plumbing, electrical work and tiling. That makes it clearer who receives questions when one stage depends on another.

Check licences, certificates and warranties

In Victoria, the published guidance says regulated plumbing and electrical work must be completed by licensed trades who issue certificates. A quote should show who is doing each part, rather than leaving every trade unnamed. Where a job combines several work types, ask whether the registered building practitioner or building company responsible for the project is identified in the paperwork.

Warranty questions should be separated into products and workmanship. Manufacturer warranties may apply to cabinets, benchtops and appliances, while the contractor's workmanship warranty concerns installation. Australian Consumer Law guarantees also apply to fixed items such as stoves, sinks, tapware and extractor fans once they are permanently part of the building. Keep the quote, certificates, product information and approved changes together so the responsibility for each item is clear.

  • Confirm the licensed trades responsible for plumbing and electrical work.
  • Ask when certificates will be issued and how they will be provided.
  • Record which warranty belongs to the manufacturer and which concerns installation.

Common questions

What affects the cost of a renovation? Kitchen size, materials and whether plumbing or electrical positions move are the main factors identified in the published guidance. Flat pack cabinets sit at a different end of the range from custom joinery with natural stone, so compare itemised scopes rather than headline totals.

How long can the work take? Timing depends on scope. Retaining the layout can finish sooner than moving plumbing or adding new walls, and custom cabinets add lead time before installation. Ask for a written schedule showing how long the kitchen will be unavailable.

Why does the trade order matter? Plumbing and electrical rough-in occur before walls close up, cabinets follow the prepared services, and tiling is a finishing stage. A named coordinator can make handovers between trades easier to track.

What should be checked before work starts? Check that the scope and price identify fittings and trades, that hidden-issue allowances are clear, and that licensed plumbing and electrical trades are named. Ask how certificates, product warranties and workmanship warranty documents will be supplied.
