Before comparing styles, inspect why the fence has failed. The post is often the deciding component. Sound posts may make a repair worthwhile, while rot near the soil line, where timber stays damp after rain, can make replacement more sensible. The website for Fencing Ipswich describes probing each post to assess whether patching is worth the money.

A site visit should establish the length of the run, what stands there now, the gate positions, the slope, rock and access. Record leaning sections, loose palings and sagging gates. Ask what work is included if the old fence is pulled out and taken away, rather than leaving removal as an assumption.

Choose the material by the job

Material selection should follow the work the fence needs to do. Colorbond steel provides privacy and a straight, low-maintenance line, and it can be stepped down a sloping yard. Timber can provide privacy between neighbours and sit comfortably beside neighbouring timber paling fences. Aluminium slat provides front screening, with spacing and finish set out in the quote.

Pool fencing needs a separate check. Glass, aluminium or tubular barriers must be arranged with Queensland pool safety rules on height, gaps and gate latches in view. A pool project therefore needs those details considered before installation, rather than treated as an ordinary boundary fence.

Account for Ipswich ground conditions

The site can change the construction method even when the fence material stays the same. Flat ground allows a continuous line, while a fall may require panels to be stepped so the fence follows the yard. Rocky ground can make each post hole slower to prepare. A rural or acreage boundary also has different construction demands from a suburban backyard because the run may be longer and needs to remain straight and tight.

The published guidance notes that Bremer River flats can hold water after heavy rain, while sloping blocks need stepped construction. Ask how post depths and footings will be settled after the site is inspected. If a retaining wall is needed first, confirm whether the fence will sit on sleeper or timber retaining work and how the combined height will be measured.

Where the combined height of a fence on a retaining wall exceeds 2 m above natural ground, the guidance says building approval will generally be needed from a building certifier before work starts. Treat that as an approval question to confirm for the particular design.

Handle repairs, removal and temporary access

A repair brief should describe the actual failure, not only the appearance. Leaning posts, loose palings and sagging gates may need different work. A replacement may include pulling out the old fence and taking it away, which should be confirmed before the job begins.

Access also affects how the job is managed. If a dog needs to remain in the yard, temporary panels may be used while new posts cure. Ask where those panels will sit, whether access through the yard is needed and how the boundary will remain closed during the work.

When neighbours share a dividing fence, the published guidance states that Queensland neighbours generally split the cost of a sufficient dividing fence equally. Use a quote checklist before approval

Comparing quotes is easier when each document answers the same questions. Look for the measured length, fence height, material, number and type of gates, ground conditions, retaining work, old-fence removal and any temporary access arrangement. A price without a site visit leaves slope, rock and gate positions unresolved.

Check that the licence suits the work and ask which parts require specialist confirmation, particularly pool barriers or retaining structures. For a sloping block, ask how the panels will step and where the finished height will be taken. For a pool barrier, ask how gaps and latch arrangements will be checked against Queensland requirements.

Common questions

How can I tell whether an old fence should be repaired? Inspect the posts first, especially at ground level. Sound posts may support repair, while rotted posts generally indicate that replacement should be considered.

Why does a sloping block need a different quote? A fence on a fall may need stepped panels, and rocky ground can make post holes slower to prepare. The site should be inspected before the construction method and price are settled.

What should I check in a fencing quote? Compare the measured run, height, material, gates, old-fence removal, retaining work and access arrangements. These details make separate quotes easier to assess.

Does a retaining wall affect fence approval? The published guidance says the combined height of a fence on a retaining wall, when above 2 m from natural ground, will generally need building approval from a building certifier. Confirm the finished design and measurement for the specific site.

What changes when the fence is beside a pool? Pool barriers need their height, gaps and gate latches considered under Queensland pool safety requirements. Confirm those details before choosing the barrier and starting work.
