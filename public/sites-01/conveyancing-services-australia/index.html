Who This Applies To

This guide suits people who know they need conveyancing help but have not settled on the right provider type. The published categories point to common situations: buying, selling, transferring ownership, buying for the first time, buying off the plan and dealing with commercial property.

  • Buyers should start with the purchase category and ask what the provider reviews before exchange or settlement steps.
  • Sellers should ask what information is needed before the provider can confirm the work and fee.
  • First home buyers can look for providers willing to explain process, terminology and quote inclusions clearly.
  • Off-the-plan buyers should ask whether the provider works with that kind of contract material.
  • Commercial parties should confirm the provider currently handles commercial transactions.

How To Compare Listings

The page invites users to read reviews, view photos, compare services and pricing, then contact providers directly by call, email or quote request. Treat those signals as preliminary filters. The final decision should rest on what the provider confirms for your file.

Compare the service label first. If a profile mentions buying, commercial work, contract matters or property transfers, ask how that experience applies to your transaction. If pricing is shown, ask what is included, what may be extra and when payment is due. If reviews are available, read for patterns in communication, clarity and follow-through instead of relying only on the headline score.

Questions Before You Choose

The page tells users to contact the business directly to confirm current scope, fees, availability and whether practitioners can assist. Turn that instruction into a short checklist before accepting a quote.

  • Which category best describes the matter: purchase, sale, transfer, first home buyer, off-the-plan, commercial or general conveyancing?
  • Who will handle the file and who will answer routine questions?
  • What work is included in the quoted fee?
  • Are searches, disbursements, settlement platform costs or third-party charges separate?
  • What information is needed before availability can be confirmed?
  • How will updates be given if another party, lender or agent needs to respond?

These questions keep the comparison anchored to the actual transaction. They also reduce the chance of comparing one provider's complete quote with another provider's narrower estimate.

Using Quote Requests Carefully

The request form described on the page is free to submit and carries no obligation to proceed. The same page says users can describe the job in about 30 seconds and do not need an account.

Write the request in plain facts. State whether you are buying, selling or transferring. Choose the closest category. Add the location, the property type if relevant and whether the matter is already under way. Avoid guessing at legal conclusions.

When responses arrive, compare scope first, then communication style and price. A lower quote still needs clear inclusions. A higher quote still needs explanation. The best next step is the provider conversation that tells you what will be done, what sits outside the fee and how the file will be managed.
