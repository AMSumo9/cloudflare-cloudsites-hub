Conveyancing Services Brisbane Explained

Brisbane Conveyancers publishes a fixed-fee property settlement model for Brisbane buyers and sellers, with Queensland Law Society-member solicitors handling legal work from contract review through to settlement day. Read that as a scope signal, not a shortcut around checking your own matter. A useful quote should identify the professional fee, likely searches, government charges, transfer duty handling, and any assumptions that could change the final cost.

The core work described is practical and document-heavy: reviewing the contract, running title and property searches, advising on conditions and key dates, arranging transfer duty, liaising with the lender and the other side, and managing settlement through PEXA. Sellers have a different document path, including contract preparation, mandatory Form 2 seller disclosure and sale settlement. Those differences matter because a buyer problem is often about conditions and searches, while a seller problem is often about disclosure and release of obligations.

Who This Applies To

This guide is for people already close to a Queensland property transaction: a first home buyer checking contract risk, a seller preparing disclosure material, an investor selling a property, an off-the-plan buyer reviewing documents before the building is complete, an auction buyer needing advice before bidding, or a commercial party dealing with leases, GST and due diligence.

  • Buyers should ask what contract clauses, searches, finance dates and settlement dates will be checked.
  • Sellers should ask how the contract and Form 2 seller disclosure will be prepared and reviewed.
  • Auction bidders should arrange contract review before bidding, because the published guidance says the cooling-off period does not apply at auction.
  • First home buyers should ask how transfer duty concessions and the First Home Owner Grant will be considered for their circumstances.

Fixed Fee Does Not Mean Every Cost Is Fixed

A fixed professional fee is useful because it tells you what the solicitor charges for a routine matter. It does not make government charges, searches, registration costs or transfer duty disappear. The published Brisbane information says disbursements are extra and that charges and transfer duty are set out separately in writing before commitment.

Before you compare two quotes, ask for the same cost categories from each provider. A lower professional fee may be less helpful if search allowances, extra contract work or settlement complications are unclear. The clean comparison is written scope against written exclusions: what is included for a standard purchase or sale, what counts as additional work, and who explains changes before they are incurred.

Timing Questions Before Contract Or Auction

Timing is where conveyancing advice becomes more than form filling. The published guide says most Queensland residential contracts have a 5 business day cooling-off period starting when the buyer receives the contract signed by both parties. It also says auction purchases do not have that cooling-off period, so contract review needs to happen before bidding rather than afterwards.

Useful questions before signing include whether finance and building inspection dates are realistic, which searches are sensible for the property, when transfer duty must be arranged, whether lender requirements may affect settlement, and whether any special conditions need attention. For off-the-plan contracts, the Brisbane source points to sunset clauses and disclosure rules as topics for solicitor review.

How Electronic Settlement Changes The Workflow

The published service description says settlement is managed through PEXA, the electronic platform used to lodge documents and transfer ownership. For a client, the important point is not the software name alone. It is that document lodgement, settlement coordination and ownership transfer are handled through an electronic process rather than a purely paper-based meeting.

That still leaves human checks. The solicitor should confirm the parties, settlement date, lender requirements, duty position and final figures. Buyers should know when funds are needed and what must happen before keys are released. Sellers should know what is required to complete the sale and how settlement proceeds will be dealt with.

Questions To Ask Before You Engage

Good conveyancing instructions start with a complete picture of the transaction. Give the solicitor the contract, property address, purchase or sale status, finance position, settlement date, and anything unusual, such as off-the-plan terms, auction timing, commercial leases or a family title transfer.

  1. Is the quote a fixed professional fee for this specific matter?
  2. Which searches, government charges and disbursements are excluded from that fee?
  3. Who provides the legal advice and are they a Queensland Law Society-member solicitor?
  4. How will contract dates, duty and lender communication be tracked?
  5. What would cause the scope or fee to change?

Those questions keep the conversation tied to the transaction rather than broad promises. They also make it easier to compare conveyancing services brisbane providers on substance: legal responsibility, written costs, document handling and settlement management.

  1. Collect the contract. Send the signed or proposed contract, property details and any agent correspondence before asking for advice.
  2. Ask for written scope. Confirm the fixed professional fee, disbursements, searches, government charges and transfer duty treatment in writing.
  3. Check key dates. Identify cooling-off, finance, inspection and settlement dates before assuming they can be changed.
  4. Clarify settlement method. Ask how PEXA settlement will be coordinated with the lender, other side and final funds.
  5. Confirm exceptions. Ask what circumstances would move the matter outside a routine fixed-fee conveyance.

Common questions

How much does Brisbane conveyancing usually cost? The published Brisbane guidance says professional fees for a routine conveyance are typically a few hundred dollars to around a thousand dollars. It also says government charges and disbursements such as title searches and registration are additional and vary by property.

Does Queensland have a cooling-off period for residential contracts? The source information says most Queensland residential contracts have a 5 business day cooling-off period from receipt of the contract signed by both parties. It also says this does not apply to auction purchases and that a penalty of up to 0.25% of the purchase price may apply if the buyer terminates during cooling-off.

What does a conveyancing solicitor do for a buyer? The published scope includes contract review, title and property searches, advice on conditions and dates, transfer duty arrangements, lender liaison and settlement management so ownership transfers correctly.

What should a first home buyer ask about? First home buyers should ask how the contract, transfer duty concessions, the First Home Owner Grant and settlement steps apply to their own purchase. The Brisbane source states the Queensland First Home Owner Grant is currently $30,000 for an eligible new home valued under $750,000 for contracts signed between 20 November 2023 and 30 June 2026, with eligibility rules applying.
