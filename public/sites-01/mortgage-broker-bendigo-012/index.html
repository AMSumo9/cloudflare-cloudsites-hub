First home buyer checks before relying on eligibility

The source page says first home buyers can discuss the Victorian First Home Owner Grant for eligible new home purchases and the federal First Home Guarantee administered by Housing Australia. It also says eligible buyers may purchase with as little as a 5 percent deposit without LMI, subject to price caps reviewed annually. Those details should be checked against current scheme rules before a property decision is made.

A useful broker conversation separates what is possible from what is approved. Ask whether the property type, deposit source, savings history, income evidence and intended purchase price fit the relevant lender and scheme settings. If the answer depends on a threshold or cap, the broker should point to the current rule rather than rely on memory.

Refinance decisions that need more than a rate comparison

The published page lists better rate, debt consolidation and accessing equity for renovation or investment as common refinance reasons. Each one creates a different test. A lower rate matters, but so do loan features, switching costs, repayment flexibility and whether the refinance changes the borrower's longer term position.

Borrowers should ask the broker to compare the current bank's offer against suitable alternatives across the available lender panel. The source page says brokers weigh variable versus fixed rate options and consider features such as offset accounts where they fit. That means a refinance discussion should show what changes, what stays the same and what the borrower gives up.

  • For rate review, ask for the repayment difference and loan feature trade-off.
  • For debt consolidation, ask how the new loan term affects total repayment behaviour.
  • For equity access, ask what evidence the lender needs for the intended use.

Investor and structure questions to settle early

The Bendigo page identifies investment property loans as a distinct scenario, including interest only versus principal and interest, offset accounts, fixed versus variable rates and how existing equity fits in. That is enough to make structure the first conversation, not an afterthought after a contract is signed.

An investor buying in Bendigo or elsewhere in regional Victoria should ask how rental income, existing debts, proposed repayments and equity position are being presented to lenders. The source page does not publish lender policy settings, so the safer approach is to ask which assumptions are being used and how they differ between lenders. A broker should be able to explain why one structure suits the borrower's goals better than another.

Interest only may be considered for cash flow or tax planning reasons, while principal and interest steadily reduces the loan balance. The right choice depends on the borrower's wider financial position, so tax advice and credit advice should not be treated as the same thing.

Timing, documents and settlement readiness

The source page says pre-approval, also called conditional approval, helps indicate how much a lender is likely to lend before house hunting. It also says typical validity is around 90 days, depending on the lender. That makes timing important for buyers who are still inspecting properties, because a stale approval may need updating before an offer becomes a live purchase.

Application to settlement is described as typically taking around 4 to 6 weeks, depending on the lender and the borrower's circumstances. Treat that range as a planning guide, not a promise. Missing payslips, unclear deposit evidence, valuation questions or a change in employment can all require more explanation before formal approval.

  1. Prepare income, identity, deposit and liability documents before lender selection.
  2. Ask what would cause the lender to reassess the file.
  3. Keep the broker informed when the property, price or settlement date changes.

Who this applies to

This guide is most relevant to borrowers in Greater Bendigo and Central Victoria who want a structured conversation before choosing a lender. The scenarios supported by the source page are first home purchase, refinancing, investment loans and pre-approval. It is less useful for readers seeking a published rate table, because the source page does not present one.

The page also suits borrowers who want to understand how broker obligations should shape the discussion. The source states that Australian residential brokers are typically paid by lender commission rather than by the borrower, that exceptions must be disclosed upfront under ASIC rules, and that mortgage brokers must act in the consumer's best interests when providing credit assistance.

  1. Define the loan goal. Decide whether the immediate task is buying, refinancing, investing or seeking pre-approval before comparing lenders.
  2. Prepare the evidence. Gather income, deposit, identity and liability details so lender options can be assessed against real information.
  3. Ask for the trade-off. Have the broker explain why a fixed, variable, offset or repayment structure suits the borrower rather than only quoting a rate.
  4. Check timing. Confirm whether the expected settlement window and any pre-approval validity still fit the property search or contract date.
