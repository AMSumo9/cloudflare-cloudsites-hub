Understanding the renovation timeline

One of the most common misunderstandings is how long the process takes. It is not an on-site week-by-week build from day one. The project starts with a design phase, followed by fabrication. Cabinetry and stone benchtops typically require a 4 to 6 week lead time because they are manufactured to specific measurements. After fabrication, the on-site phase takes another 2 to 4 weeks.

This total of 6 to 12 weeks applies to full renovations in areas like Glen Waverley or Ringwood. Makeovers are faster because they skip the structural and rough-in stages. Understanding this pipeline helps families plan their cooking and dining arrangements during the interim period. The fabrication period is largely invisible to the homeowner but is critical for quality, ensuring that components fit perfectly when installation begins.

Contracts and Victorian regulations

Victorian law mandates specific protections for homeowners. Under the Domestic Building Contracts Act 1995, any project valued over $10,000 requires a written contract. This document must detail the scope, price, and payment schedule. Additionally, domestic building insurance is generally required for work over $16,000, protecting the homeowner if the builder cannot finish the job. Compliance extends to materials as well.

Since July 2024, engineered stone containing more than 1% crystalline silica is banned. Installers now use alternatives like porcelain, sintered stone, or natural granite. Homeowners should verify these material changes with their builder to ensure safety and adherence to the renovation guide for eastern Melbourne. When reviewing contracts, look for clauses covering variations, as a fixed-price quote should remain fixed unless the homeowner changes the scope verbally, which is not recommended.

Key cost drivers

Several factors influence the final price of a project. The size of the kitchen is a primary driver, but the finish level is equally important. High-end stone benchtops and custom-made joinery cost significantly more than off-the-shelf options. Appliance packages also affect the budget. However, the biggest hidden cost can be correcting poor existing work or discovering structural issues behind walls during demolition.

This is why an initial measure-up is vital. It allows the builder to identify potential risks before providing a fixed price. While a kitchen makeover might focus on surface updates, a full renovation involves significant labour for demolition and installation, which is reflected in the quote. Understanding these drivers helps homeowners allocate funds effectively to the features that matter most.

Common questions

Do I need a building permit? A building permit is generally required if the renovation involves structural changes, removing walls, or altering plumbing and drainage. Minor cosmetic updates usually do not require permits, but it is best to confirm with your builder or local council early in the planning stage to avoid compliance issues.

What replaced engineered stone? Porcelain, sintered stone, natural granite, marble, solid timber and low-silica engineered stone are the standard alternatives. These materials provide safe and stylish options for benchtops following the ban on high-silica products.

Is a deposit required? Yes, it is standard practice to pay a deposit. In Victoria, the maximum deposit a builder can request is 10 per cent of the total contract price for work up to $20,000. This regulation ensures homeowners do not overpay before work commences.
