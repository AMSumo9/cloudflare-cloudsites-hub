Switchboard Upgrades Adelaide Explained

The guidance published by Switchboard Upgrades Adelaide treats repeated tripping as a fault-finding question before it becomes a replacement decision. The cause may sit in an appliance, old wiring or the board itself. Replacing the panel alone will not correct every source of a trip.

Record which circuit stops working, what was running at the time and whether a fuse blew or a breaker tripped. That information gives the electrician a useful starting point. Do not keep resetting a breaker without understanding why it operated. If the board has a burning smell, buzzing or hot power points nearby, the situation needs prompt attention. Smoke or sparks should be treated as an emergency and reported to 000.

  • Note the circuit or room affected.
  • Identify appliances recently added or used.
  • Tell the electrician about any visible heat, noise or damage.

What changes in an older board

A ceramic fuse box uses fuse wire that must be replaced when it blows. A modern board uses individual circuit breakers and safety switches. A breaker can be reset after the fault has been dealt with, while a safety switch cuts power quickly when current leaks and provides additional protection against serious electric shock.

A full replacement may also involve testing and labelling circuits, checking the wiring behind the panel and considering the available supply. The age of the surrounding electrical installation matters as much as the appearance of the panel, particularly where older wiring remains behind the board.

Some older boards sit on asbestos backing panels. That possibility should be raised before work starts so removal and handling can be included in the work plan. Ask what has been found, what is included in the quote and whether any further work depends on the condition of concealed wiring.

Capacity for the next appliance

A board can be technically tidy and still be unsuitable for a new electrical load. Plans for solar, a battery, an EV charger, ducted heating or induction cooking may change the required circuits and capacity. The electrician checks spare space, the existing supply and whether three-phase power or a mains upgrade needs consideration.

Spare positions in a new board create room for future circuits, but an empty position does not by itself mean that another large appliance can be added. Ask whether the supply can support the intended equipment, whether the installation needs a dedicated circuit and what changes would be required before purchase.

Build a quote that can be compared

A useful quote identifies the agreed work before the supply is disconnected. It can distinguish a complete board replacement from safety switch installation, a ceramic fuse box upgrade or fault-finding for repeated trips. It should also state whether circuit testing, labelling, asbestos considerations, old wiring and supply changes are included or excluded.

Ask the electrician to explain what is visible now and what could only be confirmed after the board is opened. Ask what has been found, what is included in the price and which work would require a separate decision. Keep the questions practical:

  • Which equipment is being removed and what replaces it?
  • Are safety switches included on the relevant circuits?
  • Will the circuits be tested and labelled?
  • Is the supply adequate for planned solar, battery, EV charging, heating or cooking equipment?
  • Will a Certificate of Compliance be provided when the work is complete?

Electrical installation work is not a home DIY task in South Australia. Replacing a switchboard belongs with the electrician, including isolation, testing and certification.

Common questions

Will the power go off during a board upgrade? Yes. The supply is isolated while the old board is replaced, so lights and appliances stop for a period. The duration depends on the board and the wiring behind it.

What is usually replaced? The old fuse or breaker panel may be replaced with a modern board containing individual circuit protection and safety switches. Wiring behind the board is also checked because it may need attention.

Does repeated tripping always mean the board needs replacement? No. The fault may be in an appliance, old wiring or the board. An electrician needs to investigate the cause before deciding whether repair or replacement is appropriate.

Will there be a Certificate of Compliance? The published guidance says the electrician provides a Certificate of Compliance after the upgrade is finished. Ask for it as part of the agreed completion documents.
