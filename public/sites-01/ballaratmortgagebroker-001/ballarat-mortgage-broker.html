How the home loan process works

Knowing the order of steps helps you tell a thorough broker from a thin one, and lets you prepare the right paperwork early.

  1. Initial discussion. Set out your goal, income, deposit and timeframe so the broker understands what you are trying to fund.
  2. Borrowing assessment. The broker reviews your finances and estimates how much you can borrow and at what loan-to-value ratio.
  3. Loan comparison. Options across the lender panel are compared on rate, fees, features and eligibility, then narrowed to a shortlist.
  4. Pre-approval. A lender confirms conditional approval, which is typically valid for around three months and lets you make offers with confidence.
  5. Application and settlement. Once you have a property, the full application is submitted, valued and progressed through to settlement.

Accreditation and the Best Interests Duty

Qualified brokers hold accreditation through industry bodies such as the Mortgage and Finance Association of Australia or the Finance Brokers Association of Australia. Since 1 January 2021, mortgage brokers have also been bound by the Best Interests Duty, a legal obligation to act in the borrower's best interests when recommending a loan. That duty is one of the clearest reasons many first home buyers choose a broker over going direct.

Frequently asked questions

Does a broker charge a fee?

In most cases the broker is paid a commission by the lender rather than a fee by the borrower, so the comparison and application service comes at no direct cost to you. Any fee that does apply must be disclosed before you proceed.

How long is a home loan pre-approval valid?

Pre-approval is typically valid for around three months. It gives you a clear budget and lets you make offers with confidence, and it can usually be refreshed if your search runs longer.

Can a broker help a first home buyer with a low deposit?

Yes. Brokers can assess low deposit and guarantor options and pathways such as the Regional First Home Buyer Guarantee and the Victorian Homebuyer Fund, then match you to a lender whose policy fits your deposit and income.
